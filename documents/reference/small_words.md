# Finding the small word

A child laughs, stops, and laughs again. Halfway through putting a morning like that into Phi, a learner looks up still, already, even, and unless, and finds no entry for any of them. English keeps a drawer of such words. Phi gives one of them a word of its own, `ture` for again, and builds the rest from words you already know.

This page is the registry for those builds. The left column is what you want to say, the middle column is how Phi makes it, and the example shows it in a sentence. Each line is settled, so two speakers who need the same small word will make it the same way.

## Saying it happens again

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| again | `ture`, directly before the verb | `shia to ture hola.` (They laughed again.) |
| another, one more | `ture`, before the noun | `mia ture soeli rinu.` (I want another cup.) |
| a second time, a third time | an ordinal with `shemu` | `shia nu wi shemu to shua.` (They came a second time.) |
| back again | the verb `turema` | `melu to turema.` (The friend came back.) |

`ture` stands with the manner words, after any particles and against the verb. `we` is a different tool. It adds someone or something else, and it has its own table below.

## Saying where things stand

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| still | `tei nosa`, with `si` on an activity | `lopia tei nosa si nulae.` (The child is still sleeping.) |
| not yet | `tei nosa` and a negated verb | `melu tei nosa ma kamo.` (The friend has not arrived yet.) |
| already | `phoe nosa`, with `ki` | `mia phoe nosa ki nuola.` (I have already eaten.) |
| no longer | `to te` | `lopia mua ha womu to te menui.` (The child no longer lives in this home.) |

Three of the four measure against `nosa`, now: until now, until now and not, before now. The fourth looks back at a stop, which is why it carries `to`. Bare `te` catches the stopping while it happens, so `pheralu te lepa` is rain easing off, and `pheralu to te lepa` is rain that no longer falls. When the story is in the past, `ra shemu` (that moment) takes over from `nosa`: `lopia tei ra shemu to si nulae.` (The child was still sleeping.)

## Saying how soon

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| at once, immediately | `pheo mu thimu` | `lo mia pheo mu thimu to wepu.` (We left at once.) |
| soon | `pheo teku shemu`, with `so` | `melu pheo teku shemu so kamo.` (The friend will arrive soon.) |
| as soon as | a `pheo` clause, then `pheo mu thimu` | `pheo melu to kamo lo mia pheo mu thimu to wepu.` (As soon as the friend arrived, we left.) |
| since then | `lue` with a time, then `tei nosa` | `mia lue ra philo tei nosa shia ma nila.` (I have not seen them since that day.) |
| since something happened | a `pheo` clause, then `tei nosa` | `pheo melu to wepu mia tei nosa ma nulae.` (I have not slept since the friend left.) |

`pheo mu thimu` is after zero time, a count that comes to nothing. It says how short the wait was. How fast the going was is `reshi`, and the two can disagree.

## Adding, excepting, replacing

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| also, too | `we` | `we lohau wile.` (The dog plays too.) |
| even | `we ko` | `we ko lopia ha shelu po theo.` (Even a child can read this book.) |
| not even | `we ko` and a negated verb | `we ko phao ha shelu ma shelomu.` (Not even the parent understands this book.) |
| everyone except, all but | `li` on the exception, verb negated | `li phao kau kowela to ma shua.` (Only the parent did not come to the council.) |
| no one except, none but | `li` on the exception | `li phao kau kowela to shua.` (Only the parent came to the council.) |
| instead | the refused thing negated, then `thona` | `mia hasumi ma milau thona phialu milau.` (I do not drink tea but water.) |

Except has no word of its own because `li` already draws the fence. Only the parent did not come, so everyone else did; only the parent came, so no one else did. Across two sentences, instead takes `whekai` where one sentence takes `thona`.

## Setting one event against another

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| when, one thing and then the next | `pheo` | `pheo melu to kamo lo mia to nuola.` (When the friend arrived, we ate.) |
| when, two things at once | `whau` | `whau melu to kamo lo mia to si nuola.` (When the friend arrived, we were eating.) |
| whenever | `lu`, with `ro` on the consequence | `lu pheralu lepa. lo mia mua womu ro manolu.` (Whenever it rains, we stay at home.) |
| until something happens | `tei` over a clause, standing first | `tei pheralu to te lepa lo mia mua womu to manolu.` (Until the rain stopped falling, we stayed at home.) |
| unless | `lu` and a negated condition | `lu pheralu ma lepa. lo mia so thalo.` (Unless it rains, we will walk.) |
| only if | `lu` with both sentences negated | `lu thia ma naphe. mia ha so po ma phoa.` (If you do not help, I will not be able to do this.) |
| even if | `shai`, with `po` in its clause | `shai pheralu po lepa lo mia so thalo.` (Even if it rains, we will walk.) |
| whether or not | `lu`, the verb and its negation joined by `sola` | `lu pheralu lepa sola ma lepa. lo mia so thalo.` (Whether it rains or not, we will walk.) |
| as if | `phea` over a relative clause and its noun | `shia phea whu ha sano miona to haolu.` (They spoke like someone who knows this.) |
| so much that | `ru` on the quality, then `thelao` | `peloru ru mioru to nai. thelao mia to seniku.` (The flower was very beautiful. So I smiled.) |
| the more, the more | `mo` in both sentences of a `lu` pair | `lu thia mo riola. thia mo shonela.` (If you work more, you learn more.) |

English when is two words wearing one coat, and Phi asks which you mean. If the friend's arrival came first and the meal after, that is `pheo`. If the meal was already on the table, that is `whau`, and the clause it opens may be the briefer of the two events. `lu` is for conditions and habits. A single past occasion under `lu` reads as if, with the speaker unsure whether it happened.
