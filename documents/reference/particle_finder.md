# Finding the particle

The particle reference and manual chapter 9 are organized by slot, which is the right order for learning the system and the wrong one for using it. A speaker in the middle of a sentence does not think "I need a Slot 1 rank four particle." They think "I want to say I heard this from someone else."

This page runs the other way. The left column is what you want to say; the middle column is the word for it. Cross-references at the end lead to the rules.

Every particle appears here exactly once, under the job it does. Thirty-eight of them, and the whole page is one sitting.

## Putting an event in time

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| it happened before now | `to` | `mia ruela to nila.` |
| it will happen | `so` | `mia ruela so nila.` |
| it is happening now | nothing | `mia ruela nila.` |

Present time is unmarked. An unmarked verb is a complete claim, not an incomplete one.

## Saying how the event unfolds

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| it is finished | `ki` | `mia ruela to ki nila.` |
| it is going on | `si` | `pheralu si lepa.` |
| it is starting | `pa` | `pheralu pa lepa.` |
| it is stopping | `te` | `pheralu te lepa.` |
| it happens as a rule | `ro` | `misheko ro nulae.` |

One of these at a time. Ending and finishing are different claims: `te` says the rain stopped, `ki` says it completed.

## Turning the sentence around

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| this was done, and the doer is beside the point | `se` | `nophi se kealo.` |
| somebody made it happen | `ka` | `phao lopia ka nulae.` |

These two are the only same-rank pair in the language, and their order is fixed: `se ka`.

## Naming where your claim comes from

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I saw or heard it myself | `hi` | `suliwa hi nai.` |
| I worked it out from evidence | `ke` | `suliwa ke nai.` |
| somebody told me | `ti` | `suliwa ti nai.` |
| I am supposing | `ho` | `suliwa ho nai.` |

Leaving all four out is not evasion. It is the ordinary way to state something you are simply prepared to assert.

## Saying what is possible or required

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| it can happen, or someone is able | `po` | `thia po shua.` |
| it has to happen | `na` | `thia na shua.` |
| it cannot | `po ma` | `thia po ma shua.` |
| it must not | `na ma` | `thia na ma shua.` |
| there is no need | the `lila` freedom frame | `thia lila wepu ralu nai.` |

Need not is the one that surprises people. It is not a particle at all; the freedom construction carries it.

## Denying

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| this did not happen, or is not so | `ma` | `pheralu ma lepa.` |

`ma` comes last in the verb's cluster, after every other particle there.

## Framing the whole utterance

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| is this so? | `wa` | `wa thia shua.` |
| do this | `no` | `no shua.` |
| if this, then that | `lu` | `lu pheralu lepa. mia so manolu.` |
| if this were so, contrary to fact | `lu he` | `lu he mia pelori nai. mia wapi.` |
| may it be so | `su` | `su thia welao nai.` |
| said with courtesy | `pi` | `pi wa thia po shua.` |

These stand at the very front, before everything, and only `pi wa`, `pi no` and `lu he` may combine.

## Measuring a degree

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| very | `ru` | `sulopa ru sulae nai.` |
| a little | `la` | `sulopa la sulae nai.` |
| enough for the purpose | `pe` | `sulopa pe sulae nai.` |
| more than the purpose allows | `wo` | `sulopa wo sulae nai.` |

One at a time, immediately before the word being measured. Nouns take them too: `ru nuhe` is much sadness, and `nuhe` has no adjective to carry the degree instead.

## Comparing

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| more than something | `mo` with `sheo` | `lohau sheo misheko mo whalo nai.` |
| more, standard understood | `mo` | `ha napa mo welao nai.` |
| most of all | `mo ko` | `ha napa mo ko welao nai.` |

Put `mo` in front of something countable and it weighs amounts rather than degrees: `mo thena` is more things.

## Pointing, singling out, adding

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| this one, near me | `ha` | `ha melu` |
| that one, over there | `ra` | `ra melu` |
| this one as against the others | `ko` | `ko mia thia nila.` |
| only this one | `li` | `li shia sano.` |
| this one too | `we` | `we lohau wile.` |

`li` fences identity and never counts. Only three eggs is a numeral's work, not `li`'s.

## Counting and ordering

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| more than one, uncounted | `lo` | `lo melu` |
| which position in a sequence | `nu` | `nu ta philo` |

A numeral or quantifier replaces `lo` rather than joining it.

## Naming a person

| You want to say | Reach for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| this word is a name | `ne` | `ne sulae` |
| and I hold them in respect | `sa` | `ne sa sulae` |
| and I am close to them | `ni` | `ne ni sulae` |
| and I regard the role they hold | `le` | `ne le sulae` |

The three honorifics record how the speaker holds the relation. None of them ranks the person named, and a bearer keeps the same name under all three.

## Where the rules live

This page names the tool. The rules that govern it are elsewhere, and you will want them once a sentence needs more than one particle at a time.

The complete inventory with slots and ranks is [the particle reference](../grammar/particle_reference.md). Manual chapter 9 explains the three scopes and how they nest, chapters 14 through 16 develop time, voice and claimed sources, and the [three slots pamphlet](../../pamphlets/three_slots/00_title.md) drills the combinations. The primer teaches every one of these in a story first, which is the shortest road for most readers.
