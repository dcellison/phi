# nitho howeli nela sileta — The North Wind and the Sun

*Aesop's fable of the wind, the sun, and the traveler's cloak is the familiar story used to show how a language sounds and how its sentences move. The 1919 Aesop for Children is the ground truth. Its complete Phi translation keeps the contest intact, from the opening quarrel to the Traveler's flight into shade and Aesop's final verdict.*

*Each passage uses a four-line block: the Phi sentence, its exact lexicon gloss, a natural English reading made from the Phi, and the source wording. A continuous Phi reading and a table of translation limits follow.*

---

## Translation

The aligned reading carries the wager from proposal through result. Every bodily action remains, as does Aesop's moral judgment. Transparent compositions do most of the lexical work, while `sharemi` (persuade) gives the moral the distinction it requires.

### The quarrel

```
nitho howeli nela sileta pha sua mo kema nai pho to shareo. lo shia to ma nawo.
north wind COORD sun INT.COMP who CMPR strong be INT.COMP.CLOSE PST discuss. PL 3SG PST NEG agree.
(The North Wind and the Sun discussed who was stronger. They did not agree.)
aesop: "The North Wind and the Sun had a quarrel about which of them was the stronger."

whau lo shia roe sheloi korua thero to si shareo nela to si theisa kapura ta whu laniru nela roe wethalu to se lomare miona nia ruela to thalo.
SIMUL PL 3SG INS MANY heart fire PST IPFV discuss COORD PST IPFV loud shout one REL journey COORD INS garment PST PASS embrace person ON path PST walk.
(While they were discussing it with much anger and shouting loudly, a traveler wrapped in a garment walked along a path.)
aesop: "While they were disputing with much heat and bluster, a Traveler passed along the road wrapped in a cloak."
```

**Notes:** The quarrel takes two ordinary acts: the Wind and Sun discuss the question, then fail to agree. The heat is `korua thero`, anger, and the bluster becomes `theisa kapura`, loud shouting. The `whau` frame keeps the traveler's walk inside that ongoing dispute. Within the traveler phrase, one predicate gives the journey and another wraps the person in `wethalu`, the general garment word whose range includes a cloak.

### The wager

```
sileta sha su lo mia tha whu lue ra laniru miona wethalu po leiro miona mo kema nai tho nawo sho to haolu.
sun QUOT.COMP OPT PL 1SG DECL.COMP REL ABL DIST journey person garment POT release person CMPR strong be DECL.COMP.CLOSE agree QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak.
(The Sun said: "May we agree that the person who can release the garment from that traveler is stronger.")
aesop: "\"Let us agree,\" said the Sun, \"that he is the stronger who can strip that Traveler of his cloak.\""

nitho howeli sha lia sho roe keloa haoni to haolu nela pheo mu thimu shia kau laniru miona whu kapura pelui kema howeli to sepho.
north wind QUOT.COMP yes QUOT.COMP.CLOSE INS rough voice PST speak COORD POST zero time 3SG ALL journey person REL shout cold strong wind PST send.
(The North Wind said "Yes" in a rough voice and immediately sent a cold, strong wind that shouted to the traveler.)
aesop: "\"Very well,\" growled the North Wind, and at once sent a cold, howling blast against the Traveler."
```

**Notes:** One quote frame carries the Sun's whole proposal, and `tha ... tho` makes the proposition the object of their hoped-for agreement. The relative clause makes the contestant the direct agent of removing the garment: `lue ... wethalu leiro`, release the garment from the traveler. Nothing in the line makes the traveler choose that release. The rough voice is how Phi approaches the growl, while `whu kapura ... howeli` is a wind that shouts. The phrase `pheo mu thimu`, after zero time, becomes immediately in natural English.

### The wind

```
roe nu ta teku kema howeli lo wethalu shuna roa laniru miona weloni to se wapho.
INS ORD one short strong wind PL garment edge AROUND journey person body PST PASS throw.
(With the first short, strong wind, the garment's edges were thrown around the traveler's body.)
aesop: "With the first gust of wind the ends of the cloak whipped about the Traveler's body."

whekai pheo mu thimu shia wethalu miso to ka noshi lomare.
CONTR POST zero time 3SG garment REFL PST CAUS near embrace.
(However, they immediately made the garment wrap closely around themself.)
aesop: "But he immediately wrapped it closely around him,"

howeli to mo kema whesu nela miona roa miso wethalu to mo noshi phelu.
wind PST CMPR strong blow COORD person AROUND REFL garment PST CMPR near hold.
(The wind blew more strongly, and the person held the garment closer around themself.)
aesop: "and the harder the Wind blew, the tighter he held it to him."

nitho howeli roe korua thero wethalu to ru kema natu.
north wind INS heart fire garment PST INTS strong pull.
(The North Wind pulled very hard at the garment in anger.)
aesop: "The North Wind tore angrily at the cloak,"

whekai shia theula mesatu whuo lureko to nai.
CONTR 3SG UNIV try WITHOUT fruit PST be.
(However, all its attempts were fruitless.)
aesop: "but all his efforts were in vain."
```

**Notes:** A gust is `teku kema howeli`, a short, strong wind. Its first impact passively throws the cloth's edges around the body. The force in Aesop's "whipped" survives without making anyone a deliberate thrower. The traveler then makes the garment embrace themself closely. The paired `mo` clauses join under `nela`: the Wind acts more strongly, and the traveler holds closer. "Tore at" describes forceful pulling rather than a completed tear; `ru kema natu` is the action and `korua thero` is the anger. The event noun made from `mesatu` names the attempts, and `whuo lureko` tells what came of them: no fruit.

### The sun

```
pheo thena sileta to pa keru loa.
POST thing sun PST INCH bright give.
(Then the Sun began to shine.)
aesop: "Then the Sun began to shine."

mua thorui shia keru loa moli to nai nela pheo nitho howeli puko pelui whu laniru miona mua welao sulae wethalu to phae nela shia nia miso lo talua wethalu to ka ralu manolu.
LOC beginning 3SG bright give gentle PST be COORD POST north wind bitter cold REL journey person LOC good warm garment PST open COORD 3SG ON REFL PL shoulder garment PST CAUS free stay.
(At first, its shining was gentle, and after the North Wind's bitter cold, the traveler opened the garment in pleasant warmth and let it remain loose on their shoulders.)
aesop: "At first his beams were gentle, and in the pleasant warmth after the bitter cold of the North Wind, the Traveler unfastened his cloak and let it hang loosely from his shoulders."

sileta keru loa mo sulae to kelu. shia mo sulae to kelu.
sun bright give CMPR warm PST become. 3SG CMPR warm PST become.
(The Sun's shining became warmer. It became warmer still.)
aesop: "The Sun's rays grew warmer and warmer."

miona miso koma wethalu to leiro. shia miso komeri to hewe.
person REFL head garment PST release. 3SG REFL forehead PST wipe.
(The person took off their head garment. They wiped their forehead.)
aesop: "The man took off his cap and mopped his brow."

pheo laeno shemu shia ru sukaro to kelu. thelao shia lue miso weloni miso wethalu to natu. shia wethalu to leiro.
POST long moment 3SG INTS hot PST become. CONS 3SG ABL REFL body REFL garment PST pull. 3SG garment PST release.
(After a long moment, they became very hot. Therefore, they pulled their garment from their body. They released it.)
aesop: "At last he became so heated that he pulled off his cloak,"

sio ruela ta shiro to whano. lila miona lue whu thape sileta keru loa phesari miona wea muila muo whu shia whelani shiro shomelu miso to wapho.
BESIDE path one tree PST stand. PURP person ABL REL burn sun bright give flee person TOWARD earth INTO REL 3SG welcome tree shadow REFL PST throw.
(A tree stood beside the path. To flee the Sun's burning light, the person threw themself down into a tree's shade, which they welcomed.)
aesop: "and, to escape the blazing sunshine, threw himself down in the welcome shade of a tree by the roadside."
```

**Notes:** The phrase `keru loa`, give brightness, covers shine, beams, and rays without leaning on the English homonym "light"; `phelo` means light in weight. One coordinated sentence keeps the traveler's two clothing actions inside the pleasant warmth. Opening the garment and letting it stay `ralu`, free, state the two parts of unfastening and loose hanging, with `lo talua` making the shoulders explicitly plural. The cap is a head garment, while `komeri` names the forehead directly. Aesop's "pulled off" becomes two motions: the traveler pulls the garment from their body, then releases it. In the last line they throw themself toward the earth rather than lie down. The explicit `miona` makes the person, not the tree in the preceding sentence, the subject of `phesari`; the final relative clause makes the traveler, rather than the shade, the one doing the welcoming.

### The moral

```
moli nela phena sharemi porua korei.
gentle COORD kind persuade purpose fulfill.
(Gentleness and kind persuasion fulfill the purpose.)
aesop: "Gentleness and kind persuasion win"

whekai kawhera nela theisa kapura porua ma korei.
CONTR coerce COORD loud shout purpose NEG fulfill.
(However, coercion and loud shouting do not fulfill the purpose.)
aesop: "where force and bluster fail."
```

**Notes:** The verb `sharemi` is persuasion, communicative influence that still leaves an answer to the listener. The adjective `phena` supplies the source's kindness rather than building it into persuasion itself. Aesop uses "win" and "fail" for success against a stated aim, so the Phi pair says that one method fulfills the purpose and the other does not. The marker `whekai` makes that contrast audible. The verb `kawhera` takes the coercive sense of force in this moral, while `theisa kapura`, loud shouting, is the audible part of bluster. The story's final flight into shade leaves Aesop's claim open to the reader's judgment.

## Complete translation

```
nitho howeli nela sileta

nitho howeli nela sileta pha sua mo kema nai pho to shareo. lo shia to ma nawo. whau lo shia roe sheloi korua thero to si shareo nela to si theisa kapura ta whu laniru nela roe wethalu to se lomare miona nia ruela to thalo.

sileta sha su lo mia tha whu lue ra laniru miona wethalu po leiro miona mo kema nai tho nawo sho to haolu. nitho howeli sha lia sho roe keloa haoni to haolu nela pheo mu thimu shia kau laniru miona whu kapura pelui kema howeli to sepho.

roe nu ta teku kema howeli lo wethalu shuna roa laniru miona weloni to se wapho. whekai pheo mu thimu shia wethalu miso to ka noshi lomare. howeli to mo kema whesu nela miona roa miso wethalu to mo noshi phelu. nitho howeli roe korua thero wethalu to ru kema natu. whekai shia theula mesatu whuo lureko to nai.

pheo thena sileta to pa keru loa. mua thorui shia keru loa moli to nai nela pheo nitho howeli puko pelui whu laniru miona mua welao sulae wethalu to phae nela shia nia miso lo talua wethalu to ka ralu manolu. sileta keru loa mo sulae to kelu. shia mo sulae to kelu. miona miso koma wethalu to leiro. shia miso komeri to hewe. pheo laeno shemu shia ru sukaro to kelu. thelao shia lue miso weloni miso wethalu to natu. shia wethalu to leiro. sio ruela ta shiro to whano. lila miona lue whu thape sileta keru loa phesari miona wea muila muo whu shia whelani shiro shomelu miso to wapho.

moli nela phena sharemi porua korei. whekai kawhera nela theisa kapura porua ma korei.
```

## Translation limits

| Aesop's wording | Phi rendering | Remaining difference |
|---|---|---|
| quarrel and disputing | discussion, disagreement, and heart-fire | Phi states the acts and feeling separately rather than using one quarrel root. |
| heat and bluster | heart-fire and loud shouting | The topic supplies the boast; Phi has no separate bluster word. |
| Traveler, man, he | `whu laniru miona`, `miona`, `shia` | The role and person survive, while grammatical gender does not. |
| road and roadside | `ruela` and a tree beside it | `ruela` covers a path or road; the English reading keeps the broader path. |
| cloak and cap | garment and head garment | Transparent composition expresses their clothing functions. |
| strip | release the garment from the Traveler | The removal is direct, though Phi uses its ordinary release verb. |
| growled and howling | rough-voiced speech and a wind that shouts | Each sound is described through an existing voice or vocalization word. |
| at once and immediately | after zero time | Phi makes the absence of delay explicit. |
| gust | short, strong wind | Duration and force compose the weather event. |
| whipped about | garment edges passively thrown around the body | The movement and force are explicit; the English cloth-motion verb has no root. |
| wrapped closely | caused the garment to embrace closely | The causative makes the Traveler the agent and the garment the encircling body. |
| the harder, the tighter | paired `mo` clauses | Phi has no correlative construction, so `nela` joins the two changes in one sentence. |
| tore angrily at | pulled very strongly with heart-fire | The action does not claim that the cloth actually tore. |
| efforts were in vain | all its attempts were without fruit | The event noun `mesatu` names the attempts without treating them as craft or labor. |
| shine, beams, and rays | give brightness | One event noun covers all three illumination terms. |
| unfastened and hung loosely | opened and caused to stay free on the shoulders | Both stages are stated without a dedicated loose-clothing root. |
| pulled off | pulled from the body, then released | Two verbs hold the source motion and result. |
| blazing sunshine | the Sun gives brightness that burns | The illumination compound receives a relative clause for burning. |
| escape and threw himself down | fled from the burning brightness and threw themself toward the earth | `phesari` states the danger-shaped departure; the reflexive action and downward direction remain explicit. |
| kind persuasion | `phena sharemi` | Kindness modifies the neutral persuasion verb. |
| win and fail | fulfill purpose and not fulfill purpose | The stated contest aim supplies the measure of success. |
| force and bluster | coercion and loud shouting | The moral's force narrows to constrained choice; bluster narrows to its audible action. |
