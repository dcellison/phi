# mophira nela lo kalora — Schleicher's Fable

*A sheep sees three horses at work. The horses answer by showing the sheep what its missing wool has become. August Schleicher wrote this small fable in reconstructed Proto-Indo-European in 1868 and supplied his own German rendering beside it. That German is stored in `texts/sources/schleicher_1868.txt`; the controlled English at `texts/sources/schleicher_1868_english.txt` contains every claim and explanatory parenthesis used here.*

*The complete Phi translation follows every claim in that English source. Schleicher's master title and the unequal burdens remain visible beside the language's material account of wool, work, and flight. Each aligned passage begins with Phi and its exact lexicon gloss, followed by natural English derived from that frozen Phi and the corresponding source lines. A continuous Phi reading and a table of translation limits follow.*

## Translation

The source leans on shearing, not cutting in general. Ecological and Work `mophiku` names the removal of an animal's fleece by cutting; `kati` alone would leave the removal unsaid. Base `tupeka` keeps the great load with its living bearer, and base `phesari` keeps escape apart from ordinary departure. Commons `phenori` gives the translation a practical relation inside Schleicher's title "master," while the exact title remains in the English witness.

### The sheep sees

```
whu mophi to ma phelu nela to ki se mophiku mophira tha ta shao kalora nai nela nu ta kalora tumoa rolutha to si natu nela nu wi kalora ta whalo tupeka to si kolua nela nu ta shao kalora miona to si reshi kolua tho to nila.
REL wool PST NEG hold COORD PST PFV PASS shear sheep DECL.COMP one three-group horse be COORD ORD one horse heavy wagon PST IPFV pull COORD ORD two horse one large burden PST IPFV carry COORD ORD one three-group horse person PST IPFV fast carry DECL.COMP.CLOSE PST see.
(A sheep that had no wool and had been shorn saw that there were three horses: the first was pulling a heavy wagon, the second was carrying one large burden, and the third was quickly carrying a person.)
schleicher-en: "A sheep on which there was no wool (a shorn sheep) saw horses:"
schleicher-en: "one pulling a heavy wagon,"
schleicher-en: "one carrying a great load,"
schleicher-en: "and one swiftly carrying a human being."
```

**Notes:** The opening relative gives both conditions to the sheep: it did not have wool and had already been shorn. Inside the proposition it sees, `ta shao kalora` establishes a group of exactly three horses. The three ordinal phrases then keep their work apart: the first pulls the wagon, the second bears the load, and the third carries the person. Imperfective `to si` makes each action ongoing at the time of the sight.

### The sheep speaks

```
mophira wei lo kalora sha whau mia tha miona lo kalora si ka wepu tho nila mia korua se peki. mia ru nuhe phaelo sho to haolu.
sheep DAT PL horse QUOT.COMP SIMUL 1SG DECL.COMP person PL horse IPFV CAUS go DECL.COMP.CLOSE see 1SG heart PASS squeeze. 1SG INTS sadness feel QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak.
(The sheep said to the horses, "While I see that a person is making the horses go, my heart is squeezed. I feel deep sadness.")
schleicher-en: "The sheep said to the horses:"
schleicher-en: "\"My heart is constricted in me (I am deeply grieved) as I see the human being driving the horses.\""
```

**Notes:** One quote frame carries the sheep's whole speech. `whau` makes the sight and the squeezed heart simultaneous, while imperfective causative `si ka wepu` presents the person as keeping the horses in motion without claiming how. The bodily image stands first. Its emotional explanation follows as intense sadness.

### The horses answer

```
lo kalora wei mophira sha kona mophira. no sheluo. lo mia ha ki nila. lo mia korua se peki. lao lo mia ha sano lo mia ru nuhe phaelo. ha miona lo mophira phenori phelu. ha miona wei miso lo mophira mophi sulae wethalu ka kelu. sheno wei lo mophira mawha mophi nai. whekai lo mophira mophi to te phelu. lo mophira se mophiku. sheno lo kalora sheo lo mophira mo towe nai sho to haolu.
PL horse DAT sheep QUOT.COMP VOC sheep. IMP listen. PL 1SG PROX PFV see. PL 1SG heart PASS squeeze. BECAUSE PL 1SG PROX know PL 1SG INTS sadness feel. PROX person PL sheep ownership hold. PROX person DAT REFL PL sheep wool warm garment CAUS become. ADD DAT PL sheep NONE wool be. CONTR PL sheep wool PST CESS hold. PL sheep PASS shear. ADD PL horse THAN PL sheep CMPR well be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak.
(The horses said to the sheep, "Sheep, listen. We have seen this. Our hearts are squeezed. Because we know this, we feel great sadness. This person holds ownership over the sheep. This person turns the sheep's wool into warm clothing for themself. In addition, the sheep have no wool. Yet the sheep stopped having wool. The sheep are shorn. The horses, moreover, are better off than the sheep.")
schleicher-en: "The horses said:"
schleicher-en: "\"Listen, sheep."
schleicher-en: "The heart is constricted in us who have seen (we are deeply grieved, because we know):"
schleicher-en: "the human being, the master, makes the sheep's wool into a warm garment for himself,"
schleicher-en: "and the sheep have no wool."
schleicher-en: "(The sheep, however, no longer have any wool; they are shorn;"
schleicher-en: "they fare even worse than the horses.)\""
```

**Notes:** The whole answer remains one act of speaking. The vocative `kona` comes before its directive, so the Phi-derived English says "Sheep, listen" where the source says "Listen, sheep." The source title *master* remains exact in its own line. Phi states the practical relation instead: this person holds `phenori`, ownership, over the sheep. A separate assertion then says what this person does with the wool, and `miso` keeps the benefit reflexive without assigning gender. Cessative `to te phelu` supplies "stopped having," and passive `mophiku` supplies shearing. The last comparison reverses because Phi has no generic word for *bad* or *worse*: the horses are more `towe`, well, than the sheep. Additive `sheno` carries the final emphasis without pretending to be a scalar word for *even*.

### The sheep flees

```
pheo mophira ha to ki hea mophira wea kosha to rato nela kau ra kosha to reshi phesari.
POST sheep PROX PST PFV hear sheep TOWARD field PST turn COORD ALL DIST field PST fast flee.
(After the sheep had heard this, it turned toward a field and quickly fled to that field.)
schleicher-en: "Having heard this, the sheep turned aside (escaped) into the field. (It made off.)"
```

**Notes:** Perfective `to ki hea` places the hearing before the departure. The sheep first turns towards a field, then flees quickly to that same field. Distal `ra kosha` recalls the field after it has been introduced, while the coordinated predicates keep the turn and flight within one departure.

## Complete translation

```
mophira nela lo kalora

whu mophi to ma phelu nela to ki se mophiku mophira tha ta shao kalora nai nela nu ta kalora tumoa rolutha to si natu nela nu wi kalora ta whalo tupeka to si kolua nela nu ta shao kalora miona to si reshi kolua tho to nila.

mophira wei lo kalora sha whau mia tha miona lo kalora si ka wepu tho nila mia korua se peki. mia ru nuhe phaelo sho to haolu.

lo kalora wei mophira sha kona mophira. no sheluo. lo mia ha ki nila. lo mia korua se peki. lao lo mia ha sano lo mia ru nuhe phaelo. ha miona lo mophira phenori phelu. ha miona wei miso lo mophira mophi sulae wethalu ka kelu. sheno wei lo mophira mawha mophi nai. whekai lo mophira mophi to te phelu. lo mophira se mophiku. sheno lo kalora sheo lo mophira mo towe nai sho to haolu.

pheo mophira ha to ki hea mophira wea kosha to rato nela kau ra kosha to reshi phesari.
```

## Translation limits

| Schleicher's English | Phi rendering | Remaining difference |
|---|---|---|
| sheep on which there was no wool | sheep that did not hold wool | Phi uses ordinary possession rather than German's locative existence. |
| pulling a heavy wagon | `tumoa rolutha natu` | The ordinary pull verb does not specify harness or traction. |
| great load | `ta whalo tupeka`, one large burden | `tupeka` centres what the horse bears; the source's broad load is slightly wider. |
| driving the horses | causing the horses to go | The causative preserves external agency but leaves its method unstated. |
| constricted heart and deep grief | squeezed heart and intense sadness | Phi separates the bodily image from Schleicher's emotional explanation. |
| Listen, sheep | Sheep, listen | Phi's extra-clausal vocative announces the addressee before the directive. |
| the master | this person holds ownership over the sheep | The exact domination title stays in the source; Phi states the practical relation it contributes here. |
| for himself | `wei miso`, for themself | Phi carries the reflexive benefit without grammatical gender. |
| fare even worse than the horses | horses are more well than sheep | Phi reverses the comparison because generic *bad* and *worse* are absent; additive `sheno` carries emphasis without marking scalar *even*. |
| turned aside, escaped, made off | turned towards a field and quickly fled to that field | Phi combines Schleicher's descriptions of one departure instead of making them separate departures. |
