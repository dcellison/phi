# thiku miona lue silero — from The Little Prince

*These three short passages come from Katherine Woods's 1943 English translation of Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's Le Petit Prince: the request for a sheep, the fox's secret, and the responsibility that follows taming. This page translates Woods's wording as it appears in those excerpts. Only the brief source lines under discussion are reproduced.*

*The source title stays in English because Phi has no ordinary word for the rank prince. The Phi title takes another route: `thiku miona lue silero`, the small person from the stars. It describes this figure within the story rather than minting a reusable rank.*

---

## Translation

The first passage is nearly all grammar. The second asks a heart to become the instrument of right seeing. The third keeps taming as taming and makes responsibility the obligation that follows it. Existing `woenu`, tame, and `naseru`, obligation, can say both plainly.

### The request

```
pi no wei mia ta mophira kire.
POL IMP DAT 1SG one sheep shape.
(Please, draw one sheep for me.)
woods: "\"If you please—draw me a sheep!\""
```

**Notes:** `pi no` is Phi's polite imperative, so the request keeps both its force and its courtesy. `wei mia` gives the requested drawing a recipient. The verb `kire` covers the stroke of a pencil as well as a hand shaping clay; its exact gloss remains shape while the English reading is draw. `ta mophira` makes Woods's indefinite sheep one sheep in Phi.

### The secret

```
sheno nosa ha mia kupela nai. ha ru siloma kupela nai.
ADD now PROX 1SG secret be. PROX INTS simple secret be.
(And now, this is my secret. It is a very simple secret.)
woods: "\"And now here is my secret, a very simple secret:"

miona li roe korua po theali nila.
person RESTR INS heart POT fitting see.
(A person can see rightly only with the heart.)
woods: "It is only with the heart that one can see rightly;"

whu noetha nai roe mirae se po ma nila.
REL essential be INS eye PASS POT NEG see.
(What is essential cannot be seen with the eye.)
woods: "what is essential is invisible to the eye.\""
```

**Notes:** Additive `sheno` keeps Woods's opening "And," while `nosa` gives the turn its now. Phi repeats the identity clause after the colon because a silent colon cannot organize a Phi utterance. Restrictive `li` settles on the heart's instrumental phrase, and `theali` makes seeing fitting or right for the purpose. The last sentence begins with a headless relative, `whu noetha nai`, so the essential itself remains the subject. Passive possibility under negation gives invisibility as something the eye cannot do.

### The responsibility

```
thia wei whu thia woenu ki ka kelu nolika naseru pa phelu. theula thimu thia ha naseru phelu.
2SG DAT REL 2SG tame PFV CAUS become animal obligation INCH hold. UNIV time 2SG PROX obligation hold.
(You begin to hold an obligation for the animal you have caused to become tame. For all time, you hold this obligation.)
woods: "\"You become responsible, forever, for what you have tamed.\""
```

**Notes:** Inside the relative clause, perfective `ki` keeps the taming complete at the present moment and causative `ka` makes the listener the cause of the animal becoming `woenu`, tame. The explicit head `nolika`, animal, closes that clause before the obligation arrives. Inchoative `pa` marks the beginning of the new relation. The second sentence then carries it through `theula thimu`, all time. `naseru` treats responsibility as an obligation under the fox's teaching; unlike `thonai`, responsible, it does not add willing acceptance.

---

## Complete translation

```
thiku miona lue silero

pi no wei mia ta mophira kire.

sheno nosa ha mia kupela nai. ha ru siloma kupela nai. miona li roe korua po theali nila. whu noetha nai roe mirae se po ma nila.

thia wei whu thia woenu ki ka kelu nolika naseru pa phelu. theula thimu thia ha naseru phelu.
```

## Translation limits

| Woods's wording | Phi treatment | Remaining distance |
|---|---|---|
| The Little Prince | source title beside `thiku miona lue silero`, small person from the stars | Phi describes the figure but does not reproduce the inherited rank. |
| If you please | polite imperative `pi no` | Woods uses a conditional courtesy; Phi marks courtesy directly on the instruction. |
| draw me a sheep | shape one sheep for me | `kire` includes drawing in its range, while its broader gloss remains shape. |
| And now here is | `sheno nosa ha ... nai`, additive now this is | Phi states the discourse addition, current time, and near-speaker identity separately. |
| a very simple secret | a second complete identity sentence | Phi repeats "this is" instead of relying on the source's colon and apposition. |
| see rightly | `theali nila`, see fittingly | `theali` judges fit to purpose and circumstance; Woods's adverb can also suggest correctness. |
| invisible to the eye | cannot be seen with the eye | Phi uses a passive action under negated possibility rather than an invisible adjective. |
| what you have tamed | the animal you have caused to become tame | The explicit animal narrows Woods's open relative but closes the Phi clause without ambiguity. |
| responsible | `naseru pa phelu`, begin to hold an obligation | Phi makes responsibility an obligation arising under the fox's teaching; willingness remains unstated. |
| forever | `theula thimu`, all time, in a second sentence | Phi repeats the obligation so the unbounded duration has a complete predicate. |
