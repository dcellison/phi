# thiku miona lue silero — Selections from The Little Prince

*These three short passages come from Katherine Woods's 1943 English translation of Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's Le Petit Prince: the request for a sheep, the fox's secret, and the responsibility that follows taming. This page translates Woods's wording as it appears in those excerpts. Only the brief source lines under discussion are reproduced.*

*The source title stays in English because Phi has no ordinary word for the rank prince. The Phi title takes another route: `thiku miona lue silero`, the small person from the stars. It describes this figure within the story rather than minting a reusable rank.*

---

## Translation

The sheep request rests mostly on Phi's grammar. In the fox's secret, the heart is the instrument of right seeing. The last passage keeps taming as taming and uses `naseru`, obligation, for the responsibility that follows.

### The request

```
pi no wei mia ta mophira kire.
[POL [IMP [[DAT 1SG] [one sheep] shape]]].
(Please draw one sheep for me.)
woods: "\"If you please—draw me a sheep!\""
```

**Notes:** `pi no` makes the request both courteous and unmistakably imperative. `wei mia` keeps the speaker as the recipient, and `ta mophira` gives the indefinite sheep a singular reading. `kire` covers a pencil giving a subject its outline as readily as a hand shaping clay; its exact gloss remains shape, while draw is the natural English here.

### The secret

```
sheno mia kupela nosa ha nai. ha kupela ru siloma nai.
[ADD [[1SG secret] now [PROX be]]]. [[PROX secret] [INTS simple] be].
(And now my secret is here. This secret is very simple.)
woods: "\"And now here is my secret, a very simple secret:"

miona li roe korua po theali nila.
[person [RESTR [INS heart]] [POT [fitting see]]].
(A person can see rightly only with the heart.)
woods: "It is only with the heart that one can see rightly;"

whu noetha nai roe mirae se po ma nila.
[REL [essential be]] [INS eye] [PASS POT NEG see].
(What is essential cannot be seen with the eye.)
woods: "what is essential is invisible to the eye.\""
```

**Notes:** `sheno` preserves Woods's opening "And," while `nosa` brings the secret into the present turn. The first sentence says that the speaker's secret is here now. The second makes that same secret very simple, since a colon cannot leave a Phi clause unfinished. Restrictive `li` belongs to the whole instrumental phrase `roe korua`, while `theali` makes the seeing right for its purpose. The headless relative `whu noetha nai` keeps the essential thing as subject; passive possibility under negation says that the eye cannot see it.

### The responsibility

```
thia wei whu thia woenu ki ka kelu nolika naseru pa phelu. thia theula thimu ha naseru phelu.
[2SG [DAT [[REL [2SG tame [PFV CAUS become]]] animal]] obligation [INCH hold]]. [2SG [UNIV time] [PROX obligation] hold].
(You begin to have an obligation to the animal you have tamed. You always have this obligation.)
woods: "\"You become responsible, forever, for what you have tamed.\""
```

**Notes:** Perfective `ki` completes the act that made the animal tame. Because the aspect belongs to the causative event, it matches Woods's "have tamed" without turning `woenu` into a verb. The head `nolika`, animal, closes the relative before `naseru`; without it, the obligation could be mistaken for the head noun. Inchoative `pa` begins the holding of that obligation. The second sentence puts `theula thimu`, all time, after the subject and says that the same obligation continues. `naseru` keeps responsibility as an obligation under the fox's teaching; `thonai` would add willing acceptance that Woods does not state.

---

## Complete translation

```
thiku miona lue silero

pi no wei mia ta mophira kire.

sheno mia kupela nosa ha nai. ha kupela ru siloma nai. miona li roe korua po theali nila. whu noetha nai roe mirae se po ma nila.

thia wei whu thia woenu ki ka kelu nolika naseru pa phelu. thia theula thimu ha naseru phelu.
```

## Translation limits

| Woods's wording | Phi treatment | Remaining distance |
|---|---|---|
| The Little Prince | source title beside `thiku miona lue silero`, small person from the stars | Phi describes the figure but does not reproduce the inherited rank. |
| If you please | polite imperative `pi no` | Woods uses a conditional courtesy; Phi marks courtesy directly on the instruction. |
| draw me a sheep | shape one sheep for me | `kire` includes drawing in its range, while its broader gloss remains shape. |
| And now here is | `sheno mia kupela nosa ha nai`, my secret is here now | Phi states the discourse addition, current time, and near-speaker location separately. |
| a very simple secret | `ha kupela ru siloma nai`, this secret is very simple | Phi gives the apposition its own complete sentence instead of asking a colon to hold it open. |
| see rightly | `theali nila`, see in a fitting way | `theali` judges fit to purpose and circumstance; Woods's adverb can also suggest correctness. |
| invisible to the eye | cannot be seen with the eye | Phi uses a passive action under negated possibility rather than an invisible adjective. |
| what you have tamed | the animal you have caused to become tame | The explicit animal narrows Woods's open relative, but it also gives the Phi relative an audible end before `naseru`. |
| responsible | `naseru pa phelu`, begin to hold an obligation | Phi makes responsibility an obligation arising under the fox's teaching; willingness remains unstated. |
| forever | `thia theula thimu ha naseru phelu`, you always have this obligation | Phi repeats the obligation in a complete sentence and gives the unbounded duration adverbial scope. |
