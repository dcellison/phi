# thiku miona lue silero — from The Little Prince

*Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's Le Petit Prince is the most-translated book outside scripture, and language-builders have a particular affection for it: the fox's secret may be the most-rendered sentence in constructed languages. These are Phi's excerpts: three short passages from Katherine Woods's 1943 translation, each transmuted with notes. The book remains in copyright, so the units carry no source-citation lines and the English appears only in these short quoted excerpts.*

*The first choice is in the title. "Prince" stays in the source as a rank Phi does not lexicalize. The Phi title describes the figure within the story instead: `thiku miona lue silero`, the small person from the stars, a child tending a small world alone.*

---

## The request

> *"If you please—draw me a sheep!"*

```
pi no wei mia ta mophira kire.
POL IMP DAT 1SG one sheep shape.
(Please, draw me a sheep.)
```

**Notes:** This is the book's most famous request. The sheep is `mophira`, whose sound echoes `mophi`, wool, without reducing the animal to its fleece. It is the same animal Schleicher's fable finds shorn. Drawing is one of `kire`'s acts: a pencil gives the sheep its outline, just as a hand gives clay a curve. The gloss remains shape, but its natural English here is "draw." The politeness stack `pi no` is the canonical gentle imperative. Woods's "If you please" is its source, and the small person is insistent without being rude.

## The secret

> *"And now here is my secret, a very simple secret: It is only with the heart that one can see rightly; what is essential is invisible to the eye."*

```
nosa ha mia kupela nai. ru siloma kupela.
now PROX 1SG secret be. INTS simple secret.
(Now, this is my secret. A very simple secret.)

miona li roe korua po theali nila.
person RESTR INS heart POT fitting see.
(Only with the heart can one see rightly.)

whu noetha nai roe mirae se po ma nila.
REL essential be INS eye PASS POT NEG see.
(What is essential cannot be seen with the eye.)
```

**Notes:** `nosa` restores the turn in Woods's "And now." Her "very simple secret" takes `siloma`, simple. `mueli` belongs to an undecorated bowl; information has no painted rim to leave bare. The intensifier stays with simplicity in the nominal fragment `ru siloma kupela`. "Rightly" is `theali`, fitting, and `li` gives "only" to the instrument of the heart. The last sentence keeps what is essential as its subject. Instrumental `roe mirae` supplies the singular eye, while passive potential and negation say that the essential cannot be seen with it.

## The responsibility

> *"You become responsible, forever, for what you have tamed."*

```
theula thimu. thia wei whu thia nua ki nolami thonai kelu.
UNIV time. 2SG DAT REL 2SG COM PFV bond responsible become.
(Forever: you willingly take responsibility for the one with whom you have bonded.)
```

**Notes:** The fox explains taming by the ties it creates, so this transmutation keeps `nolami`, bond. Exact taming could be described as causing an animal to become `woenu`, accustomed to human presence, but this telling chooses a mutual relation instead. The comitative gap remains audible in `whu thia nua ___ ki nolami`, the one with whom you have bonded. Perfective `ki` keeps the completed bond present when responsibility begins, as Woods's "have tamed" requires. All time, `theula thimu`, stands first as its own fragment. `thonai` is responsibility willingly taken up. The derived English says so plainly instead of hiding the choice inside "responsible."

## What the transmutation changed

*Gap log: prince → retained in the source title while the Phi title becomes `thiku miona lue silero`, the small person from the stars; draw → `kire`, shape or draw; and now → current-time `nosa`; a very simple secret → `ru siloma kupela`, simplicity under the intensifier; see rightly → `theali nila`, see fittingly, under possibility `po`; invisible to the eye → `whu noetha nai roe mirae se po ma nila`, what is essential cannot be seen with the eye; tame → exact `woenu ka kelu` is set aside for mutual `nolami`, bond, under the fox's own explanation; what you have tamed → the one with whom you have bonded; have tamed → perfective `ki nolami`; forever → `theula thimu`, all time; become responsible → `thonai kelu`, willingly take responsibility. New words coined: none.*
