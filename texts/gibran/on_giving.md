# loa — On Giving

*A person with many possessions asks about giving. Gibran answers with a prudent dog, a full well, an orchard, and gifts that become wings. The Phi translation keeps the whole teaching. Its praise of giving everything stays, as does Gibran's language of pain, merit, and divine parentage.*

*Each passage uses a four-line block: the Phi sentence, its exact lexicon gloss, a literal English back-translation, and Gibran's source wording. A continuous Phi reading and a table of translation limits follow.*

---

## Close translation

This is the whole teaching, from the rich man's request to the freehearted earth and God. Wealth, baptism, myrtle, and God remain descriptions in Phi beside their exact source wording. Two recurring moral relations receive direct module words: `parelu` names a judgement of deservingness without confusing it with inherent worth, and `thaweno` names a reward without turning it into a gift or agreed exchange.

### Possession, tomorrow, and fear

```
ta himo [whu lo lorua thena phelu] miona sha no wei lo mia nia loa haolu sho to haolu.
one HUM.CLF [REL PL abundant thing hold] person QUOT.COMP IMP DAT PL 1SG ON give speak QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak.
(A person who held abundant things said, "Speak to us about giving.")
gibran: "Then said a rich man, Speak to us of _Giving_."

phirae miona to lonae.
different person PST respond.
(A different person answered.)
gibran: "And he answered:"

lu thia lo [whu mua thia phenori nai] thena loa. thia li thiku loa.
COND 2SG PL [REL LOC 2SG ownership be] thing give. 2SG RESTR small give.
(When you give things that are in your ownership, you give only a little.)
gibran: "You give but little when you give of your possessions."

lu thia miso loa. thia shewo loa.
COND 2SG REFL give. 2SG true give.
(When you give yourself, you truly give.)
gibran: "It is when you give of yourself that you truly give."

lao thia tha mua wireo philo lo shia so po phelona tho remo. thia mua sukima lo [whu mua thia phenori nai] thena phelu nela shorupo. thia lo [whu mua thia phenori nai] thena li hina nai.
BECAUSE 2SG DECL.COMP LOC future day PL 3SG FUT POT require DECL.COMP.CLOSE think. 2SG LOC fear PL [REL LOC 2SG ownership be] thing hold COORD protect. 2SG PL [REL LOC 2SG ownership be] thing RESTR what be.
(Because you think that you may require them on a future day, you hold and protect in fear the things that are in your ownership. What are your possessions except these things?)
gibran: "For what are your possessions but things you keep and guard for fear you may need them tomorrow?"

wireo philo wei [whu mua [whu mawha ruela phelu] kirua lo tholau kupe nela noe lo [whu kau thunepa laniru] miona thalo] wo thesa lohau hina so thola.
future day DAT [REL LOC [REL NONE path hold] sand PL bone hide COORD BEHIND PL [REL ALL sacred place journey] person walk] EXC careful dog what FUT bring.
(What will a future day bring to the excessively careful dog who hides bones in pathless sand and walks behind people journeying to a sacred place?)
gibran: "And tomorrow, what shall tomorrow bring to the overprudent dog burying bones in the trackless sand as he follows the pilgrims to the holy city?"

wa phelona sukima li phelona nai.
Q require fear RESTR require be.
(Is fear of need only need itself?)
gibran: "And what is fear of need but need itself?"

lu thia phitura pheno nai. wa kishu sukima [whu se po ma heno] kishu nai.
COND 2SG well full be. Q thirsty fear [REL PASS POT NEG satisfy] thirsty be.
(If your well is full, is fear of thirst a thirst that cannot be satisfied?)
gibran: "Is not dread of thirst when your well is full, the thirst that is unquenchable?"
```

**Notes:** The rich man becomes a person who holds abundant things; `simoe` cannot rank a person by possessions because its richness belongs to soil, colour, flavour, and similar depth. Ownership is stated with Commons `phenori` rather than smuggled into `phelu`, ordinary holding. The dog hides bones in sand that holds no path and walks behind people journeying to a sacred place. That material description preserves burial, following, pilgrimage, and holy city without giving any of them a source-specific title. The two final questions keep Gibran's identification of fear with need and thirst. Their presence here reports his claim rather than making fear reliable evidence.

### The givers and their motives

```
soli [whu lo lorua thena phelu] miona lo thiku thena loa. lila shia se miratu. shia lo shia thena loa. shia [whu se kupe] rinu lo shia loami peloma ka kelu.
SOME [REL PL abundant thing hold] person PL small thing give. PURP 3SG PASS recognize. 3SG PL 3SG thing give. 3SG [REL PASS hide] want PL 3SG gift harmful CAUS become.
(Some people who hold abundant things give small things. So that they may be recognized, they give their things. Their hidden desire makes their gifts harmful.)
gibran: "There are those who give little of the much which they have--and they give it for recognition and their hidden desire makes their gifts unwholesome."

soli miona li lo thiku thena phelu. shia theula shia thena loa.
SOME person RESTR PL small thing hold. 3SG UNIV 3SG thing give.
(Some people hold only a few things. They give all their things.)
gibran: "And there are those who have little and give it all."

ha lo miona lioru nela lioru lorua nohero. lo shia phelure molu mawha thimu whemoa nai.
PROX PL person life COORD life abundant believe. PL 3SG store container NONE time empty be.
(These people believe in life and life's abundance. Their storage container is never empty.)
gibran: "These are the believers in life and the bounty of life, and their coffer is never empty."

soli miona roe siora loa. ha siora lo shia thaweno nai.
SOME person INS joy give. PROX joy PL 3SG reward be.
(Some people give with joy. This joy is their reward.)
gibran: "There are those who give with joy, and that joy is their reward."

soli miona roe kipona loa. ha kipona lo shia thoepa phialu thorea nai.
SOME person INS pain give. PROX pain PL 3SG sacred water ceremony be.
(Some people give with pain. This pain is their sacred water ceremony.)
gibran: "And there are those who give with pain, and that pain is their baptism."

soli miona loa. shia mua loa kipona ma sano. shia siora ma sheraki. shia roe taleri thesua ma loa.
SOME person give. 3SG LOC give pain NEG know. 3SG joy NEG search. 3SG INS virtuous mindful NEG give.
(Some people give. In giving, they do not know pain. They do not seek joy, and they do not give with mindfulness of virtue.)
gibran: "And there are those who give and know not pain in giving, nor do they seek joy, nor give with mindfulness of virtue;"

shia phea [whu mua ra lewonu muo tholu welao whinu leiro] wotami loa.
3SG AS [REL LOC DIST valley INTO space good smell release] shrub give.
(They give like a shrub in that valley releasing a pleasant smell into space.)
gibran: "They give as in yonder valley the myrtle breathes its fragrance into space."

thoepa phiora thue ha lo miona lo manuwe haolu. shia noe lo shia mirae nia muila seniku.
sacred spirit THROUGH PROX PL person PL hand speak. 3SG BEHIND PL 3SG eye ON earth smile.
(The sacred spirit speaks through these people's hands. From behind their eyes, it smiles upon the earth.)
gibran: "Through the hands of such as these God speaks, and from behind their eyes He smiles upon the earth."
```

**Notes:** Recognition, hidden desire, pain, and virtue all remain separate from the transfer itself. `thaweno` gives reward its own relation, while the sacred water ceremony describes baptism without claiming to reproduce its inherited identity. Myrtle is a named species in the source and a shrub in Phi. Its action is the release of a pleasant smell rather than literal respiration; the source retains Gibran's personifying verb. God is a sacred spirit within the Phi line. The source citation keeps the identity that the description cannot.

### Asking, searching, and holding

```
lu thia sha pi wa thia po loa sho hea. thia loa welao nai. lu thia whuo ha haolu roe shelomu loa. thia loa mo welao nai.
COND 2SG QUOT.COMP POL Q 2SG POT give QUOT.COMP.CLOSE hear. 2SG give good be. COND 2SG WITHOUT PROX speak INS understand give. 2SG give CMPR good be.
(If you hear, "Could you please give?", your giving is good. If you give without this speech, through understanding, your giving is better.)
gibran: "It is well to give when asked, but it is better to give unasked, through understanding;"

heloi miona [whu loami so howela] miona sheraki. ha sheraki sheo loa mo siora nai.
generous person [REL gift FUT receive] person search. PROX search THAN give CMPR joy be.
(A generous person searches for someone who will receive a gift. This search is more joyful than giving.)
gibran: "And to the open-handed the search for one who shall receive is joy greater than giving."

thia hina phelu rinu.
2SG what hold want.
(What do you want to keep?)
gibran: "And is there aught you would withhold?"

theula whu thia phelu so se loa.
UNIV REL 2SG hold FUT PASS give.
(All that you hold will be given.)
gibran: "All you have shall some day be given;"

lila thia loa meluna phelu nela lo thia lumira ma phelu. no nosa loa.
PURP 2SG give season hold COORD PL 2SG descendant NEG hold. IMP now give.
(So that you may hold the season of giving and your descendants may not hold it, give now.)
gibran: "Therefore give now, that the season of giving may be yours and not your inheritors’."
```

**Notes:** Phi has no general request verb. The first condition therefore lets the listener hear a polite question, while `whuo ha haolu`, without this speech, makes the unasked gift audible. The generous person's search is an event noun and the comparative follows Gibran without deciding whether the receiver wishes to be found. Holding renders withholding and lets the question remain as broad as the source. Descendants stand for inheritors because legal succession remains with its source.

### Deservingness and receiving

```
thia sha mia so loa thona mia li wei [whu loami parelu] miona so loa sho ro haolu.
2SG QUOT.COMP 1SG FUT give ADVRS 1SG RESTR DAT [REL gift deserve] person FUT give QUOT.COMP.CLOSE HAB speak.
(You often say, "I will give, but I will give only to a person who deserves a gift.")
gibran: "You often say, “I would give, but only to the deserving.”"

lo shiro mua thia lureko shiro koshira senao ma haolu. lo mophira mua thia whelina kosha senao ma haolu.
PL tree LOC 2SG fruit tree grove same NEG speak. PL sheep LOC 2SG grass field same NEG speak.
(The trees in your orchard do not say the same. The sheep in your pasture do not say it either.)
gibran: "The trees in your orchard say not so, nor the flocks in your pasture."

shia liona porua phelu. shia loa. lu shia loa naweri. shia lumeo.
3SG live purpose hold. 3SG give. COND 3SG give refuse. 3SG die.
(They hold the purpose of living, and they give. If they refuse to give, they die.)
gibran: "They give that they may live, for to withhold is to perish."

[whu lo philo nela lo shero howela parelu] miona lue thia theula phirae thena howela we parelu.
[REL PL day COORD PL night receive deserve] person ABL 2SG UNIV different thing receive ALSO deserve.
(A person who deserves to receive days and nights also deserves to receive every other thing from you.)
gibran: "Surely he who is worthy to receive his days and his nights, is worthy of all else from you."

[whu lue lioru thaluro milau parelu] miona lue thia thiku phuliro miso soeli pheno ka kelu parelu.
[REL ABL life ocean drink deserve] person ABL 2SG small stream REFL cup full CAUS become deserve.
(A person who deserves to drink from life's ocean deserves to make their own cup full from your little stream.)
gibran: "And he who has deserved to drink from the ocean of life deserves to fill his cup from your little stream."

thomari nela norethi nela heloi howela parelu sheo hina mo whalo nai.
courage COORD confident COORD generous receive deserve THAN what CMPR large be.
(The deservingness of receiving with courage, confidence, and generosity is greater than what?)
gibran: "And what desert greater shall there be, than that which lies in the courage and the confidence, nay the charity, of receiving?"

lila thia [whu tawemi ma phelu] lo shia rolia nila nela [whu nupira ma phelu] lo shia thoru nila. wa lo miona lo miso pharoe na phae nela lo miso thoru na sirelu. thia sua nai.
PURP 2SG [REL cover NEG hold] PL 3SG worth see COORD [REL shame NEG hold] PL 3SG proud see. Q PL person PL REFL chest NEC open COORD PL REFL proud NEC disclose. 2SG who be.
(So that you may see their uncovered worth and their pride without shame, must people open their own chests and disclose their own pride? Who are you?)
gibran: "And who are you that men should rend their bosom and unveil their pride, that you may see their worth naked and their pride unabashed?"

no mua thorui tha thia loa miona nela loa tenoa parelu tho somela.
IMP LOC beginning DECL.COMP 2SG give person COORD give tool deserve DECL.COMP.CLOSE observe.
(First observe that you deserve to be a giving person and a tool of giving.)
gibran: "See first that you yourself deserve to be a giver, and an instrument of giving."
```

**Notes:** `parelu` is a direct word for a reported judgement of desert. It assigns neither inherent `rolia` worth nor a right. Entitlement and qualification answer different questions in their own entries. The orchard uses the registered `lureko shiro koshira`, fruit-tree grove, and the pasture is a grass field. Gibran's courage, confidence, and charity become courage, confidence, and generosity in receiving. His naked worth has no cover, and his unabashed pride has no shame. The chest is opened rather than given a root for rending. In the final line, a giver is a giving person and an instrument is a giving tool.

### Life, gratitude, and wings

```
whau thia tha miso loa miona nai tho remo. lioru wei lioru loa. thia li [whu somela] miona nai.
SIMUL 2SG DECL.COMP REFL give person be DECL.COMP.CLOSE think. life DAT life give. 2SG RESTR [REL observe] person be.
(While you think that you are the giver, Life gives to Life. You are only a person who observes.)
gibran: "For in truth it is life that gives unto life--while you, who deem yourself a giver, are but a witness."

kona lo [whu howela] miona. theula thia howela miona nai. lila thia wei miso nela wei [whu loa] miona tupeka ma thinu. no nemo pamolu pilu.
VOC PL [REL receive] person. UNIV 2SG receive person be. PURP 2SG DAT REFL COORD DAT [REL give] person burden NEG place. IMP grateful weight take.
(Receivers: all of you are receiving people. So that you do not place a burden on yourself and on the person who gives, do not take up a weight of gratitude.)
gibran: "And you receivers--and you are all receivers--assume no weight of gratitude, lest you lay a yoke upon yourself and upon him who gives."

no nua [whu loa] miona phea lo wapile nia lo shia loami rihe.
IMP COM [REL give] person AS PL wing ON PL 3SG gift rise.
(Rise with the person who gives, upon their gifts as upon wings.)
gibran: "Rather rise together with the giver on his gifts as on wings;"

lu thia thia naseru wo thesua nai. thia [whu phea phao ralu korua muila phelu nela phea phao thoepa phiora phelu] miona heloi whamoi.
COND 2SG 2SG obligation EXC mindful be. 2SG [REL AS parent free heart earth hold COORD AS parent sacred spirit hold] person generous doubt.
(If you are excessively mindful of your obligation, you doubt the generosity of a person who holds the freehearted earth as a parent and the sacred spirit as a parent.)
gibran: "For to be overmindful of your debt, is to doubt his generosity who has the freehearted earth for mother, and God for father."
```

**Notes:** Simultaneous `whau` lets the person's belief remain present while Life gives. A witness is a person who observes. Gibran's receivers become a direct address before `theula thia`, all of you, makes everyone a receiver. Debt is a socially attributed obligation, `naseru`, not a number or contract silently reconstructed in Phi. The final relative clause keeps both parents but not their gender: freehearted earth and sacred spirit are each held as `phao`, parent.

## Complete translation

```
ta himo [whu lo lorua thena phelu] miona sha no wei lo mia nia loa haolu sho to haolu. phirae miona to lonae.

lu thia lo [whu mua thia phenori nai] thena loa. thia li thiku loa. lu thia miso loa. thia shewo loa. lao thia tha mua wireo philo lo shia so po phelona tho remo. thia mua sukima lo [whu mua thia phenori nai] thena phelu nela shorupo. thia lo [whu mua thia phenori nai] thena li hina nai. wireo philo wei [whu mua [whu mawha ruela phelu] kirua lo tholau kupe nela noe lo [whu kau thunepa laniru] miona thalo] wo thesa lohau hina so thola. wa phelona sukima li phelona nai. lu thia phitura pheno nai. wa kishu sukima [whu se po ma heno] kishu nai.

soli [whu lo lorua thena phelu] miona lo thiku thena loa. lila shia se miratu. shia lo shia thena loa. shia [whu se kupe] rinu lo shia loami peloma ka kelu. soli miona li lo thiku thena phelu. shia theula shia thena loa. ha lo miona lioru nela lioru lorua nohero. lo shia phelure molu mawha thimu whemoa nai. soli miona roe siora loa. ha siora lo shia thaweno nai. soli miona roe kipona loa. ha kipona lo shia thoepa phialu thorea nai. soli miona loa. shia mua loa kipona ma sano. shia siora ma sheraki. shia roe taleri thesua ma loa. shia phea [whu mua ra lewonu muo tholu welao whinu leiro] wotami loa. thoepa phiora thue ha lo miona lo manuwe haolu. shia noe lo shia mirae nia muila seniku.

thia sha mia so loa thona mia li wei [whu loami parelu] miona so loa sho ro haolu. lo shiro mua thia lureko shiro koshira senao ma haolu. lo mophira mua thia whelina kosha senao ma haolu. shia liona porua phelu. shia loa. lu shia loa naweri. shia lumeo. [whu lo philo nela lo shero howela parelu] miona lue thia theula phirae thena howela we parelu. [whu lue lioru thaluro milau parelu] miona lue thia thiku phuliro miso soeli pheno ka kelu parelu. thomari nela norethi nela heloi howela parelu sheo hina mo whalo nai. lila thia [whu tawemi ma phelu] lo shia rolia nila nela [whu nupira ma phelu] lo shia thoru nila. wa lo miona lo miso pharoe na phae nela lo miso thoru na sirelu. thia sua nai. no mua thorui tha thia loa miona nela loa tenoa parelu tho somela.

whau thia tha miso loa miona nai tho remo. lioru wei lioru loa. thia li [whu somela] miona nai. kona lo [whu howela] miona. theula thia howela miona nai. lila thia wei miso nela wei [whu loa] miona tupeka ma thinu. no nemo pamolu pilu. no nua [whu loa] miona phea lo wapile nia lo shia loami rihe. lu thia thia naseru wo thesua nai. thia [whu phea phao ralu korua muila phelu nela phea phao thoepa phiora phelu] miona heloi whamoi.
```

## Translation limits

| Source concept | Phi treatment | Boundary retained |
|---|---|---|
| rich man | a person who holds abundant things | `simoe` does not rank people by possessions, and no legal or economic class is inferred |
| possessions and coffer | things in an ownership relation; a storage container | property rules, wealth, and the exact object remain with the source |
| overprudent dog and pilgrims | an excessively careful dog; people journeying to a sacred place | burial and following are described, while pilgrimage and holy-city identities remain unclaimed |
| reward and deservingness | `thaweno`; `parelu` | reward remains distinct from gift and exchange; desert remains a judgement distinct from worth, right, entitlement, and qualification |
| baptism | sacred water ceremony | the inherited rite and its theological force stay in Gibran's line |
| myrtle | shrub | the source preserves the named species; Phi preserves the valley, pleasant smell, and release into space |
| God | sacred spirit | the source alone supplies the divine identity, pronoun, and parentage |
| inheritors | descendants | exact inheritance relations and legal succession remain outside the Phi claim |
| naked worth and unabashed pride | worth without a cover; pride without shame | the physical and emotional images remain, but English homonymy does not create a Phi root |
| debt, mother, and father | obligation; parent | exact debt and gendered kinship remain in the source wording |
