# riola — On Work

*Gibran's farmer asks about work, and the answer runs from earth's pace to the voices of day and night. The Phi translation lets him finish that argument, hard edges and all.*

*Each passage uses a four-line block: the Phi sentence, its exact lexicon gloss, a literal English back-translation, and Gibran's source wording. A continuous Phi reading and a table of translation limits follow.*

---

## Close translation

This is the whole teaching, from the farmer's request to the voices of day and night. The translation follows Gibran into his praise of work, his hierarchy of crafts, and his stern judgment of labour without love. Phi does not have to agree before it can listen accurately.

### Earth and the shared procession

```
ta himo [whu kosha sorila] telo sha no wei lo mia nia riola haolu sho to haolu.
one HUM.CLF [REL field cultivate] farmer QUOT.COMP IMP DAT PL 1SG ON labor speak QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak.
(A farmer who cultivates a field said, "Speak to us about work.")
gibran: "Then a ploughman said, Speak to us of _Work_."

phirae miona to lonae.
different person PST respond.
(A different person answered.)
gibran: "And he answered, saying:"

lila thia nua muila nela muila norea thimelo po phelu thia riola.
PURP 2SG COM earth COORD earth soul pace POT hold 2SG labor.
(You work so that you may keep pace with earth and the soul of earth.)
gibran: "You work that you may keep pace with the earth and the soul of the earth."

lu miona ma riola. shia wei lo meluna kuewa miona kelu. lu shia ma riola. shia lue lioru nuawe laniru pholeni. lioru nuawe laniru wea [whu mawha lumae phelu] ruela thunoa wepu. shia roe thoru ha ruela kulo kelomi.
COND person NEG labor. 3SG DAT PL season strange person become. COND 3SG NEG labor. 3SG ABL life together journey depart. life together journey TOWARD [REL NONE end hold] path dignified go. 3SG INS proud PROX path guide accept.
(If a person does not work, they become a stranger to the seasons. If they do not work, they depart from life's shared journey. Life's shared journey goes with dignity towards a path that has no end. With pride, it accepts this path's guidance.)
gibran: "For to be idle is to become a stranger unto the seasons, and to step out of life’s procession, that marches in majesty and proud submission towards the infinite."

lu thia riola. thia phui nai. thimu whishela thue thia korua meliphe kelu.
COND 2SG labor. 2SG flute be. time whisper THROUGH 2SG heart music become.
(When you work, you are a flute. Time's whisper becomes music through your heart.)
gibran: "When you work you are a flute through whose heart the whispering of the hours turns to music."

whau theula phirae thena nuawe koru meliho thoa lo thia sua [whu mawha haoni phelu] maeli weoli po nai.
SIMUL UNIV different thing together harmonious sing AMONG PL 2SG who [REL NONE voice hold] quiet reed POT be.
(While all other things sing together in harmony, who among you could be a quiet reed with no voice?)
gibran: "Which of you would be a reed, dumb and silent, when all else sings together in unison?"

wei thia tha riola [whu lue peloma haolu shua] tupeka nai nela riola peloma soleha nai tho to ro se ti haolu.
DAT 2SG DECL.COMP labor [REL ABL harmful speak come] burden be COORD labor harmful luck be DECL.COMP.CLOSE PST HAB PASS REP speak.
(You have long been told that work is a burden arising from harmful speech, and that labour is misfortune.)
gibran: "Always you have been told that work is a curse and labour a misfortune."

whekai mia wei thia haolu. lu thia riola. thia mua muila mo ko wuero whemura ta porua korei. whau ha whemura to thowia ha porua wei thia to se rilowa.
CONTR 1SG DAT 2SG speak. COND 2SG labor. 2SG LOC earth CMPR FOC far dream one purpose fulfill. SIMUL PROX dream PST born PROX purpose DAT 2SG PST PASS assign.
(But I say this to you. When you work, you fulfil one purpose within earth's furthest dream. When this dream was born, this purpose was assigned to you.)
gibran: "But I say to you that when you work you fulfil a part of earth’s furthest dream, assigned to you when that dream was born,"

lu thia nua riola manolu. thia shewo lioru lothea.
COND 2SG COM labor stay. 2SG true life love.
(When you remain with labour, you truly love life.)
gibran: "And in keeping yourself with labour you are in truth loving life,"

lu thia thue riola lioru lothea. thia lioru mo ko nulo kupela nulo sano.
COND 2SG THROUGH labor life love. 2SG life CMPR FOC deep secret deep know.
(When you love life through labour, you know life's deepest secret deeply.)
gibran: "And to love life through labour is to be intimate with life’s inmost secret."
```

**Notes:** The opening farmer is a cultivator of a field rather than the operator of a named plough. `thimelo phelu`, hold pace, preserves the bodily rhythm of Gibran's first answer. The idle person is simply one who does not work, `ma riola`; the close translation does not smuggle in `wesha`, lazy, and its judgment belongs to Gibran. His procession becomes `lioru nuawe laniru`, life's shared journey. It goes with dignity towards an endless path and accepts that path's guidance with pride. Gibran's wording keeps the religious and hierarchical force of submission. Phi's natural time vocabulary has no hours, so `thimu whishela`, time's whisper, carries the flute image without importing the clock. A curse becomes a burden arising from harmful speech, while misfortune is harmful luck. One purpose within the dream stands for one part of it. Intimacy with the secret becomes deep knowledge, a closeness of understanding rather than an unmarked social relation.

### Pain and the ladder

```
lu whekai thia kipona phaelo. thia tha thowia peloma nai nela mashuri panoru [whu nia thia komeri se thekiro] tupeka nai tho haolu. mia wei thia lonae. li thia komeri siwe [whu se thekiro] thena so sawa.
COND CONTR 2SG pain feel. 2SG DECL.COMP born harmful be COORD flesh support [REL ON 2SG forehead PASS write] burden be DECL.COMP.CLOSE speak. 1SG DAT 2SG respond. RESTR 2SG forehead sweat [REL PASS write] thing FUT wash.
(But if you feel pain, you say that birth is harmful and the support of flesh is a burden written on your forehead. I answer you: only the sweat of your forehead will wash what is written.)
gibran: "But if you in your pain call birth an affliction and the support of the flesh a curse written upon your brow, then I answer that naught but the sweat of your brow shall wash away that which is written."

wei thia tha lioru nuelo nai tho we to se ti haolu. lu thia shorui nai. thia lo shorui miona haolu thole.
DAT 2SG DECL.COMP life dark be DECL.COMP.CLOSE ALSO PST PASS REP speak. COND 2SG weary be. 2SG PL weary person speak practice.
(You have also been told that life is darkness. When you are weary, you rehearse the speech of weary people.)
gibran: "You have been told also that life is darkness, and in your weariness you echo what was said by the weary."

whekai mia haolu. lu mawha rinu nai. lioru shewo nuelo nai.
CONTR 1SG speak. COND NONE want be. life true dark be.
(But I say this: when there is no desire, life truly is dark.)
gibran: "And I say that life is indeed darkness ‘save when there is urge,"

lu mawha sano nai. theula rinu mawha keiro nila.
COND NONE know be. UNIV want NONE way see.
(When there is no knowledge, all desire sees no way.)
gibran: "And all urge is blind save when there is knowledge,"

lu mawha riola nai. theula sano poru ma nai.
COND NONE labor be. UNIV know productive NEG be.
(When there is no work, all knowledge is not productive.)
gibran: "And all knowledge is vain save when there is work,"

lu mawha lothea nai. theula riola whemoa nai.
COND NONE love be. UNIV labor empty be.
(When there is no love, all work is empty.)
gibran: "And all work is empty save when there is love;"

lu thia roe lothea riola. thia miso nela lo phirae miona nela thoepa phiora lorea.
COND 2SG INS love labor. 2SG REFL COORD PL different person COORD sacred spirit connect.
(When you work with love, you connect yourself, other people, and the sacred spirit.)
gibran: "And when you work with love you bind yourself to yourself, and to one another, and to God."
```

**Notes:** Gibran's brow stays a forehead, and his writing remains visible through a passive relative clause. `thole`, practise or rehearse, gives the weary person's echo an action without treating Phi as though it had an acoustic metaphorical root for repeating an opinion. His `urge` becomes ordinary `rinu`, wanting. Blind desire sees no way; vain knowledge is not productive. These are descriptions of the ladder, not quiet revisions of it. In the last line, `lorea` connects rather than restrains. `thoepa phiora`, sacred spirit, describes a religious referent but does not reproduce the identity of Gibran's God. That identity remains exact in the adjacent source line.

### Work done with love

```
roe lothea riola hina nai.
INS love labor what be.
(What is work done with love?)
gibran: "And what is it to work with love?"

lu he [whu thia meropi] miona ha wetha so wenuha. thia roe [whu lue thia korua se natu] lo sima wetha selomi.
COND IRR [REL 2SG cherish] person PROX cloth FUT wear. 2SG INS [REL ABL 2SG heart PASS pull] PL thread cloth weave.
(If the person you cherish were to wear this cloth, you weave it with threads drawn from your heart.)
gibran: "It is to weave the cloth with threads drawn from your heart, even as if your beloved were to wear that cloth."

lu he [whu thia meropi] miona mua ha womu so menui. thia roe noeli lothea ha womu lorima.
COND IRR [REL 2SG cherish] person LOC PROX home FUT dwell. 2SG INS warm (emotional) love PROX home build.
(If the person you cherish were to dwell in this home, you build it with warm love.)
gibran: "It is to build a house with affection, even as if your beloved were to dwell in that house."

lu he [whu thia meropi] miona ha lureko so nuola. thia roe phaeli lo thinoe thophe. thia roe siora lureko muraki.
COND IRR [REL 2SG cherish] person PROX fruit FUT eat. 2SG INS tender PL seed sow. 2SG INS joy fruit harvest.
(If the person you cherish were to eat this fruit, you sow seeds tenderly and harvest fruit with joy.)
gibran: "It is to sow seeds with tenderness and reap the harvest with joy, even as if your beloved were to eat the fruit."

thia theula [whu thia kire] thena roe thia phiora ta whunei pheno ka kelu.
2SG UNIV [REL 2SG shape] thing INS 2SG spirit one breathe full CAUS become.
(You fill all things you shape with one breath of your spirit.)
gibran: "It is to charge all things you fashion with a breath of your own spirit,"

thia tha theula [whu to lumeo nela to se thorai] miona roa thia whano nela somela tho sano.
2SG DECL.COMP UNIV [REL PST die COORD PST PASS bless] person AROUND 2SG stand COORD observe DECL.COMP.CLOSE know.
(You know that all people who died and were blessed stand around you and observe.)
gibran: "And to know that all the blessed dead are standing about you and watching."

mia lue thia phea [whu nulae] miona tha [whu kerou theko nela mua kerou miso norea kire hekawi] miona sheo [whu muila sorila] miona mo thunoa nai tho ro ti hea.
1SG ABL 2SG AS [REL sleep] person DECL.COMP [REL stone craft COORD LOC stone REFL soul shape find] person THAN [REL earth cultivate] person CMPR dignified be DECL.COMP.CLOSE HAB REP hear.
(I often hear from you, as from a sleeping person, that one who crafts stone and finds their own soul's shape in it is more dignified than one who cultivates earth.)
gibran: "Often have I heard you say, as if speaking in sleep, “He who works in marble, and finds the shape of his own soul in the stone, is nobler than he who ploughs the soil."

mia lue thia tha [whu loriphi kema pilu nela nia wetha phea miona loriphi welisha kire] miona sheo [whu wei lo mia lo palowe theko] miona mo thunoa nai tho we ro ti hea.
1SG ABL 2SG DECL.COMP [REL rainbow strong take COORD ON cloth AS person rainbow color shape] person THAN [REL DAT PL 1SG PL shoe craft] person CMPR dignified be DECL.COMP.CLOSE ALSO HAB REP hear.
(I also often hear from you that one who forcefully takes the rainbow and shapes its colours on cloth in a person's likeness is more dignified than one who crafts shoes for us.)
gibran: "And he who seizes the rainbow to lay it on a cloth in the likeness of man, is more than he who makes the sandals for our feet.”"

whekai mua thaeso mia ma nulae. mia ru selua nai. howeli wei lo whalo shiro kaelo haolu. shia wei mo ko thiku whelina senao keiro haolu.
CONTR LOC midday 1SG NEG sleep. 1SG INTS aware be. wind DAT PL large tree sweet speak. 3SG DAT CMPR FOC small grass same way speak.
(But at midday I am not asleep. I am intensely aware. The wind speaks sweetly to large trees, and it speaks in the same way to the smallest grass.)
gibran: "But I say, not in sleep but in the overwakefulness of noontide, that the wind speaks not more sweetly to the giant oaks than to the least of all the blades of grass;"

li [whu howeli haoni melira ka kelu] miona thunoa nai. shia lothea ha melira mo kaelo ka kelu.
RESTR [REL wind voice song CAUS become] person dignified be. 3SG love PROX song CMPR sweet CAUS become.
(Only the person who turns the wind's voice into song is dignified. Their love makes this song sweeter.)
gibran: "And he alone is great who turns the voice of the wind into a song made sweeter by his own loving."
```

**Notes:** Phi asks the question without `wa`: `hina`, what, is the one gap. Gibran's reasons then move before the actions they explain. Each irrealis condition says who might use the cloth, home, or fruit before the making begins. `meropi` leaves the beloved as a person who is cherished rather than inventing a permanent person class. Spirit enters through one breath, and the dead stand and observe in a framed proposition. Marble narrows to stone; ploughing to cultivation; sandals to the broad `palowe`, shoe. `kema pilu`, take forcefully, keeps the rainbow's sudden capture before its colours reach the cloth. The comparison supplies `thunoa`, dignified, where the second half of Gibran's quotation leaves the quality understood. Oaks become large trees, while the least blade becomes the smallest grass. Midday is the natural station `thaeso`, not a clock reading.

### Love made visible

```
riola [whu se po nila] lothea nai.
labor [REL PASS POT see] love be.
(Work is love that can be seen.)
gibran: "Work is love made visible."

lu thia roe lothea po ma riola nela li whuo kaeli riola. thia lue riola pholeni. thia mua thunepa maewo meilo. thia lue [whu roe siora riola] miona loami howela. ha keiro sheo whuo kaeli riola mo welao nai.
COND 2SG INS love POT NEG labor COORD RESTR WITHOUT like labor. 2SG ABL labor depart. 2SG LOC sacred place threshold sit. 2SG ABL [REL INS joy labor] person gift receive. PROX way THAN WITHOUT like labor CMPR good be.
(If you cannot work with love and labour only without liking it, depart from the work. Sit at the threshold of a sacred place and receive gifts from people who work with joy. This course is better than labour without liking it.)
gibran: "And if you cannot work with love but only with distaste, it is better that you should leave your work and sit at the gate of the temple and take alms of those who work with joy."

lu thia whuo theama napa napako. thia [whu miona li thiku lumera] puko napa napako.
COND 2SG WITHOUT care bread bake. 2SG [REL person RESTR small nourish] bitter bread bake.
(If you bake bread without care, you bake bitter bread that nourishes a person only a little.)
gibran: "For if you bake bread with indifference, you bake a bitter bread that feeds but half man’s hunger."

lu thia lo lowi mirulo peki ma kaeli. thia phaelo lureko milau pekira ka kelu.
COND 2SG PL vine berry squeeze NEG like. 2SG feel fruit drink toxic CAUS become.
(When you do not like squeezing vine berries, your feeling makes the fruit drink toxic.)
gibran: "And if you grudge the crushing of the grapes, your grudge distils a poison in the wine."

lu thia phea lo thoepa phiora meliho nela thia meliho ma lothea. thia philo haoni nela shero haoni wei lo miona hewasu te ka kelu.
COND 2SG AS PL sacred spirit sing COORD 2SG sing NEG love. 2SG day voice COORD night voice DAT PL person audible CESS CAUS become.
(If you sing like sacred spirits and do not love the singing, you make the voices of day and night cease to be audible to people.)
gibran: "And if you sing though as angels, and love not the singing, you muffle man’s ears to the voices of the day and the voices of the night."
```

**Notes:** `riola [whu se po nila] lothea nai` preserves Gibran's best-known line without adding a verb for visibility. The final judgment stays. The temple becomes the threshold of a sacred place, and alms become gifts received from joyful workers. Indifference is absence of care; the forbidden fraction becomes `li thiku`, only a little. Distaste becomes labour without liking, not the stronger recoil of `kophinu`, disgust. Grapes are vine-berries, and wine follows the established `lureko milau`, fruit drink. The worker's feeling makes the drink toxic. Gibran's distillation stays in his line. Angels are described as sacred spirits. The worker still causes the voices to cease being audible; Gibran keeps the physical image of muffled ears.

```

riola [whu se po nila] lothea nai. lu thia roe lothea po ma riola nela li whuo kaeli riola. thia lue riola pholeni. thia mua thunepa maewo meilo. thia lue [whu roe siora riola] miona loami howela. ha keiro sheo whuo kaeli riola mo welao nai. lu thia whuo theama napa napako. thia [whu miona li thiku lumera] puko napa napako. lu thia lo lowi mirulo peki ma kaeli. thia phaelo lureko milau pekira ka kelu. lu thia phea lo thoepa phiora meliho nela thia meliho ma lothea. thia philo haoni nela shero haoni wei lo miona hewasu te ka kelu.
```

## Translation limits

| Gibran wording | Phi treatment | Remaining difference |
|---|---|---|
| ploughman | a farmer who cultivates a field | The role and worked land remain. Phi does not name the plough or say that one was being used at that moment. |
| idle; stranger to the seasons; life's procession | not working; becoming unfamiliar to the seasons; departing from life's shared journey | Conditional clauses unfold Gibran's equivalence. `ma riola` does not add the moral judgment of lazy, and `laniru` is a journey rather than an ordered procession. |
| majesty and proud submission | dignity; accepting the endless path's guidance with pride | The posture and direction survive. Submission's religious and hierarchical force stays in Gibran's wording rather than entering Phi as a generic virtue. |
| hours | `thimu`, time | Phi does not divide the day into clock hours. The adjacent source preserves Gibran's measured image. |
| curse; misfortune | a burden arising from harmful speech; harmful luck | The two judgments remain distinct. A tradition's claimed mechanism of cursing stays with the source. |
| a part of earth's furthest dream | one purpose within earth's furthest dream | Purpose makes the contribution intelligible, but a purpose is not simply a part. |
| intimate with life's inmost secret | know life's deepest secret deeply | The closeness becomes depth of understanding. English intimate also suggests a relation that the Phi does not separately assert. |
| God | `thoepa phiora`, sacred spirit | The description marks a religious referent. Gibran's exact divine identity remains solely in his source line. |
| marble; giant oaks | stone; large trees | Material and scale remain, while the particular stone and tree species do not. |
| seizes the rainbow; lays it on cloth | forcefully takes the rainbow; shapes its colours on cloth | The force of capture survives, while laying gives way to the artistic action that follows. |
| nobler; more; great | more dignified; dignified | `thunoa` names dignity displayed in a person or their manner. It narrows Gibran's broader language of comparative stature rather than treating physical size as greatness. |
| temple gate; alms | threshold of a sacred place; gifts from joyful workers | Sacred setting and received sustenance remain. The institutional building and charitable practice stay with Gibran. |
| half a man's hunger | nourish a person only a little | Phi has no fraction word for half, and it leaves gender unstated. The qualitative shortfall remains. |
| distaste; grudge | labour without liking it; not liking the crushing | Phi states the aversion without escalating either feeling into `kophinu`, disgust. Gibran's stronger shades stay beside the Phi. |
| grapes; wine; distils poison | vine-berries; fruit drink; the worker's feeling makes the drink toxic | The ingredients, feeling, and result are visible. Gibran's source supplies the fermentation, distillation, and named wine. |
| angels; muffle ears | sacred spirits; making the voices cease to be audible | The religious beings are described, and the worker still causes the loss of hearing. The physical image of muffled ears stays in the source. |
