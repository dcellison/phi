# lothea — On Love

*Almitra asks about love, and Gibran answers with wings that hide a sword and a garden caught by the north wind. Later, grain passes under the millstone and bread enters sacred fire. The close translation includes the whole teaching, wounds and all. The transmutation listens to the same images and considers what love may invite without making suffering its proof.*

*Both renderings use four-line blocks: the Phi sentence, its exact lexicon gloss, a literal English back-translation, and Gibran's source wording. Their comparison follows the places where consent, injury, fear, and shared bread turn the second rendering onto another path.*

---

## Close translation

This is the whole teaching, from Almitra's request to the prayer and song at night. The translation describes Gibran's sword and crown without adding roots for them. Crucifixion, threshing, and sacred feast receive the same treatment. The source says all it has to say before Phi answers.

### The summons and the wings

```
ta himo miona sha no wei lo mia nia lothea haolu sho to haolu.
one HUM.CLF person QUOT.COMP IMP DAT PL 1SG ON love speak QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak.
(A person said, "Speak to us about love.")
gibran: "Then said Almitra, Speak to us of _Love_."

phirae miona miso koma to ka rihe. shia lo miona to somela. lo miona maeli to kelu. shia roe whalo haoni to haolu.
different person REFL head PST CAUS rise. 3SG PL person PST observe. PL person quiet PST become. 3SG INS large voice PST speak.
(A different person raised his head and observed the people. The people became quiet. He spoke with a great voice.)
gibran: "And he raised his head and looked upon the people, and there fell a stillness upon them. And with a great voice he said:"

lu lothea thia whola. no lothea ruela thalo.
COND love 2SG invite. IMP love path walk.
(When love invites you, walk love's path.)
gibran: "When love beckons to you, follow him,"

whekai lo shia ruela kethua nai. lo shia ruela reshi rihe.
CONTR PL 3SG path hard be. PL 3SG path fast rise.
(However, its paths are hard. Its paths rise quickly.)
gibran: "Though his ways are hard and steep."

lu shia lo wapile thia lomare. no shia lomare kelomi.
COND 3SG PL wing 2SG embrace. IMP 3SG embrace accept.
(When its wings embrace you, accept its embrace.)
gibran: "And when his wings enfold you yield to him,"

whekai [whu thoa shia lo wapile se kupe] tiso laeno keluo thena wei thia kaworu po loa.
CONTR [REL AMONG 3SG PL wing PASS hide] sharp long metal thing DAT 2SG injury POT give.
(However, a long sharp metal thing hidden among its wings may give you an injury.)
gibran: "Though the sword hidden among his pinions may wound you."
```

**Notes:** Almitra's name remains in the source line; Phi uses `ta himo miona`, one person, because an unadapted name cannot occupy a Phi sentence. The beckon is rendered as an invitation, and following it as walking love's path. A steep path is one whose height rises quickly, so `reshi rihe` states the bodily fact without a new adjective. No neutral word for sword is added. Here it is a long sharp metal thing hidden among wings, and its possible wound is `kaworu`, an injury rather than a decorative pain.

### Voice, crown, branch, and root

```
lu shia wei thia haolu. no shia theomi.
COND 3SG DAT 2SG speak. IMP 3SG trust.
(When it speaks to you, trust it.)
gibran: "And when he speaks to you believe in him,"

whekai shia haoni lo thia whemura ka po pukate. senao keiro nitho howeli thepalu ka po kema pukeri.
CONTR 3SG voice PL 2SG dream CAUS POT break. same way north wind garden CAUS POT strong damage.
(However, its voice may cause your dreams to break, just as the north wind may severely damage a garden.)
gibran: "Though his voice may shatter your dreams as the north wind lays waste the garden."

lothea roe [whu nia koma se wenuha] keluro thena thia so keluro. shia wi themo wolea tiwa. senao keiro lothea nia ha wolea tei thia lumeo thia so ka phelu. lothea thia so ka thuroa. senao keiro shia lo thia loremi so kati.
love INS [REL ON head PASS wear] honor thing 2SG FUT honor. 3SG two THING.CLF wood tie. same way love ON PROX wood UNTIL 2SG die 2SG FUT CAUS hold. love 2SG FUT CAUS grow. same way 3SG PL 2SG branch FUT cut.
(Love will honour you with a sign of honour worn on the head. It ties two pieces of wood together. In the same way, love will cause you to be held on this wood until you die. Love will cause you to grow, and it will also cut your branches.)
gibran: "For even as love crowns you so shall he crucify you. Even as he is for your growth so is he for your pruning."

shia kau thia raeli so kalei. shia lo [whu mua sileta phelo tiripe] mo ko phaeli loremi so moli palo.
3SG ALL 2SG height FUT climb. 3SG PL [REL LOC sun light quiver] CMPR FOC tender branch FUT gentle touch.
(It will climb to your height. It will gently touch your most tender branches, which quiver in sunlight.)
gibran: "Even as he ascends to your height and caresses your tenderest branches that quiver in the sun,"

shia lue thia raeli kau lo thia tumeki so wepu. lo thia tumeki muila kema phelu. shia lo thia tumeki so ka tiripe.
3SG ABL 2SG height ALL PL 2SG root FUT go. PL 2SG root earth strong hold. 3SG PL 2SG root FUT CAUS quiver.
(It will go from your height to your roots. Your roots hold the earth strongly. It will cause your roots to quiver.)
gibran: "So shall he descend to your roots and shake them in their clinging to the earth."

shia kau miso thia phea [whu roe nira se tiwa] napuro so sholei.
3SG ALL REFL 2SG AS [REL INS cord PASS tie] grain FUT gather.
(It will gather you to itself as grain tied with cord.)
gibran: "Like sheaves of corn he gathers you unto himself."
```

**Notes:** Gibran tells the listener to believe in a personified love, so Phi uses `theomi`, trust, rather than `nohero`, belief that a proposition is true. The north wind damages the garden severely; `whemori`, resource waste, does not belong to a ruined bed of plants. Crown and crucifixion are rebuilt in material steps. A crown is a head-worn sign of honour. Crucifixion ties two pieces of wood and holds a body there until death. Their symmetry is still present, but neither object gains a reusable Phi name. Growth uses causative `thuroa`; pruning is the cutting of branches. Love then climbs, touches, descends, and makes the roots quiver while their hold on earth is visible.

### Grain, fire, and the heart

```
lila shia lue thia thia tawemi so leiro shia thia so si kema palo.
PURP 3SG ABL 2SG 2SG cover FUT release 3SG 2SG FUT IPFV strong touch.
(So that it may release your covering from you, it will continue forceful contact with you.)
gibran: "He threshes you to make you naked."

lila shia lue thia lo [whu thia tawemi] thena so leiro shia thue mera thia so ka roke.
PURP 3SG ABL 2SG PL [REL 2SG cover] thing FUT release 3SG THROUGH net 2SG FUT CAUS move.
(So that it may release from you the things that cover you, it will move you through a net.)
gibran: "He sifts you to free you from your husks."

shia muo whilo thia so kuremi.
3SG INTO white 2SG FUT grind.
(It will grind you into whiteness.)
gibran: "He grinds you to whiteness."

shia thia so lupaki. shia thia so pesa. ha teru tei luwi so manolu.
3SG 2SG FUT fold. 3SG 2SG FUT push. PROX process UNTIL flexible FUT stay.
(It will fold you and press you. This process will continue until there is flexibility.)
gibran: "He kneads you until you are pliant;"

lila thia wei thoepa phiora thoepa holia nurome thoepa napa kelu shia muo shia thoepa thero thia so sepho.
PURP 2SG DAT sacred spirit sacred festival meal sacred bread become 3SG INTO 3SG sacred fire 2SG FUT send.
(So that you may become sacred bread for the sacred spirit's sacred festival meal, it will send you into its sacred fire.)
gibran: "And then he assigns you to his sacred fire, that you may become sacred bread for God’s sacred feast."

lila thia thia korua lo kupela sano lothea ha theula thena wei thia so phoa. thelao thia lioru korua thiku thena so kelu.
PURP 2SG 2SG heart PL secret know love PROX UNIV thing DAT 2SG FUT do. CONS 2SG life heart small thing FUT become.
(So that you may know the secrets of your heart, love will do all these things to you. Therefore you will become a small thing of Life's heart.)
gibran: "All these things shall love do unto you that you may know the secrets of your heart, and in that knowledge become a fragment of Life’s heart."
```

**Notes:** Phi has no thresh, sift, husk, or knead root. Each operation is visible. Continuing forceful contact loosens the covering, and a net separates by size. Grinding makes the grain white; folding and pressure make the mixture flexible. Gibran's purpose must precede the action it explains, so the sacred bread comes before the sending into fire. `thoepa phiora`, sacred spirit, describes the divine referent while the source line preserves God. The fragment of Life's heart is `lioru korua thiku thena`, a small thing of Life's heart. The part-whole image survives without pretending that `phanoi`, portioning, names a broken piece.

### Fear and sufficient love

```
lu thia mua sukima li lothea shea nela lothea nirelo hekawi thueli. thelao wei thia mo welao nai. no roe wetha thia weloni tawemi. no lue lothea napuro riola tomae pholeni.
COND 2SG LOC fear RESTR love peace COORD love pleasant find intend. CONS DAT 2SG CMPR good be. IMP INS cloth 2SG body cover. IMP ABL love grain labor floor depart.
(If, in fear, you intend to find only love's peace and pleasure, then this is better for you: cover your body with cloth and depart from love's grain-working floor.)
gibran: "But if in your fear you would seek only love’s peace and love’s pleasure, Then it is better for you that you cover your nakedness and pass out of love’s threshing-floor,"

thia muo [whu mawha meluna phelu] lokue so koema. thia so hola. whekai thia hola whoa so ma nai. thia so nuhewa. whekai thia nuhewa whoa so ma nai.
2SG INTO [REL NONE season hold] place FUT enter. 2SG FUT laugh. CONTR 2SG laugh whole FUT NEG be. 2SG FUT weep. CONTR 2SG weep whole FUT NEG be.
(You will enter a place that holds no season. You will laugh, but your laughter will not be whole. You will weep, but your weeping will not be whole.)
gibran: "Into the seasonless world where you shall laugh, but not all of your laughter, and weep, but not all of your tears."

lothea li miso loa. lothea li lue miso pilu.
love RESTR REFL give. love RESTR ABL REFL take.
(Love gives only itself; love takes only from itself.)
gibran: "Love gives naught but itself and takes naught but from itself."

lothea ma phelu. lothea se ma phelu.
love NEG hold. love PASS NEG hold.
(Love holds nothing, and is not held.)
gibran: "Love possesses not nor would it be possessed;"

lothea lothea heno.
love love satisfy.
(Love satisfies love.)
gibran: "For love is sufficient unto love."
```

**Notes:** The conditional preserves Gibran's judgment intact. Seeking is stated as intending to find, with love's peace and pleasure as the restricted objects. Nakedness is made physical through cloth covering the body, and the threshing-floor is the floor where grain work happens. The seasonless world is a place that holds no season. Its laughter and weeping still occur, but neither is whole. The next three claims need no enlargement. Love gives itself, cannot be possessed, and satisfies itself in Phi's shortest clauses of the teaching.

### Direction and desire

```
lu thia lothea. no sha thoepa phiora mua mia korua nai sho ma haolu. no sha mia mua thoepa phiora korua nai sho haolu.
COND 2SG love. IMP QUOT.COMP sacred spirit LOC 1SG heart be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE NEG speak. IMP QUOT.COMP 1SG LOC sacred spirit heart be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE speak.
(When you love, do not say, "The sacred spirit is in my heart." Say, "I am in the heart of the sacred spirit.")
gibran: "When you love you should not say, “God is in my heart,” but rather, “I am in the heart of God.”"

no tha thia lothea ruela po kulo tho ma remo. lu lothea tha thia rolia phelu tho hekawi. lothea thia ruela kulo.
IMP DECL.COMP 2SG love path POT guide DECL.COMP.CLOSE NEG think. COND love DECL.COMP 2SG worth hold DECL.COMP.CLOSE find. love 2SG path guide.
(Do not think that you can guide love's path. If love finds that you hold worth, love guides your path.)
gibran: "And think not you can direct the course of love, for love, if it finds you worthy, directs your course."

lothea li tha miso pheno kelu tho pula.
love RESTR DECL.COMP REFL full become DECL.COMP.CLOSE wish.
(Love wishes only that it become full.)
gibran: "Love has no other desire but to fulfil itself."

lu thia lothea nela lo pula na phelu. su ha lo pula thia lo pula nai.
COND 2SG love COORD PL wish NEC hold. OPT PROX PL wish 2SG PL wish be.
(If you love and must hold desires, may these desires be your desires.)
gibran: "But if you love and must needs have desires, let these be your desires:"

su thia lue patoku muo larewu kelu. su thia phea thiku luphore nai. ha luphore selu. shia wei shero miso melira meliho.
OPT 2SG ABL solid INTO liquid become. OPT 2SG AS small river be. PROX river flow. 3SG DAT night REFL song sing.
(May you become liquid from solid. May you be like a small river. This river flows and sings its own song to the night.)
gibran: "To melt and be like a running brook that sings its melody to the night."
```

**Notes:** Within Phi, God is a sacred spirit; Gibran's line still says God. `rolia phelu`, hold worth, renders worthy without suggesting that love creates the worth it finds. Phi guides rather than directs, so the two paths preserve Gibran's reversal without a commander's hand. Fullness supplies fulfilment. The modal `na` makes "must needs" audible before the optative turns the desires into wishes. Melting is a change from solid `patoku` to liquid `larewu`; the brook then receives its own flow and song.

### Tenderness and the natural day

```
su thia wo phaeli kipona sano.
OPT 2SG EXC tender pain know.
(May you know the pain of too much tenderness.)
gibran: "To know the pain of too much tenderness."

su thia lue miso lothea shelomu kaworu howela.
OPT 2SG ABL REFL love understand injury receive.
(May you receive an injury from your own understanding of love.)
gibran: "To be wounded by your own understanding of love;"

su thia roe pula nela siora lorika leiro.
OPT 2SG INS wish COORD joy blood release.
(May you willingly and joyfully release blood.)
gibran: "And to bleed willingly and joyfully."

su thia mua horathe roe wapile korua rihe. su thia lao we ta lothea philo sha mea sho haolu.
OPT 2SG LOC dawn INS wing heart rise. OPT 2SG BECAUSE ALSO one love day QUOT.COMP thanks QUOT.COMP.CLOSE speak.
(May you rise at dawn with a wing-heart, and say "thanks" for one more day of loving.)
gibran: "To wake at dawn with a winged heart and give thanks for another day of loving;"

su thia mua philo kesho therilu. su thia mua lothea whalo siora phirena.
OPT 2SG LOC day middle rest. OPT 2SG LOC love large joy meditate.
(May you rest at the day's middle, and meditate within love's great joy.)
gibran: "To rest at the noon hour and meditate love’s ecstacy;"

su thia mua howai nemo kau womu turema.
OPT 2SG LOC evening grateful ALL home return.
(May you return home at evening with gratitude.)
gibran: "To return home at eventide with gratitude;"

su thia nulae. su mua thia korua wei [whu thia meropi] miona thorai nai. su nia thia phulae wei thoepa phiora woraka meliho nai.
OPT 2SG sleep. OPT LOC 2SG heart DAT [REL 2SG cherish] person bless be. OPT ON 2SG mouth DAT sacred spirit appreciate sing be.
(Then may you sleep, with a blessing in your heart for the person you cherish and a song of appreciation for the sacred spirit upon your lips.)
gibran: "And then to sleep with a prayer for the beloved in your heart and a song of praise upon your lips."
```

**Notes:** The close translation does not soften the final cost. Excessive tenderness has pain, understanding gives injury, and willingness and joy accompany released blood. `wapile korua`, wing-heart, recalls the opening image when dawn returns. Phi's natural day supplies dawn, the day's middle, and evening without importing clock units. `whalo siora` renders ecstasy as love's great joy. Prayer and praise have no universal roots: a blessing for the cherished person rests in the heart, while a song of appreciation for the sacred spirit rests on the mouth.

### Translation limits

| Gibran wording | Phi treatment | Remaining difference |
|---|---|---|
| Almitra; he and him | one person asks and a different person answers; `shia` supplies the third person | Phi does not admit an unadapted name into its syntax and does not mark the source's gendered pronouns. |
| beckons; follow; steep | love invites, the listener walks its path, and the path rises quickly | The invitation is less commanding than beckoning followed by obedience. Fast rise describes the slope without a direct steep adjective. |
| sword hidden among pinions | a long sharp metal thing hidden among wings | The mechanism and danger remain, but only the source gives the thing its weapon name. |
| crowns; crucifies | a head-worn sign of honour; two pieces of wood on which a person is held until death | Material description preserves both images without granting either a general Phi root. |
| pruning | cutting the branches | The purpose and horticultural judgment are supplied by the source rather than by a specialised pruning verb. |
| threshes; naked; sifts; husks; kneads | continuing forceful contact, removal of coverings, movement through a net, and repeated folding and pressure | The sequence is visible, though its five English labels remain in the source. |
| God; sacred feast | a sacred spirit and that spirit's sacred festival meal | Phi can describe the religious referent but cannot preserve the source identity inside the sentence. |
| fragment of Life's heart | a small thing of Life's heart | The part-whole relation survives without a noun for a broken fragment. |
| But if | the condition opens with `lu`, and its consequence begins with `thelao` | Slot 0 `lu` must open its sentence, so the preceding contrast is carried by the passage rather than an added coordinator. |
| prayer; praise; ecstacy | a blessing, a song of appreciation for the sacred spirit, and great joy | The close rendering describes the acts and feeling while the source retains its devotional categories and spelling. |

## Transmutation

The transmutation follows every source movement, but it does not make injury a credential of love. The invitation can be refused, and fear can speak. Grain passes through human hands and becomes bread for a shared table, while no person is sent into the fire.

### Invitation and consent

```
ta himo miona sha no wei lo mia nia lothea haolu sho to haolu.
one HUM.CLF person QUOT.COMP IMP DAT PL 1SG ON love speak QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak.
(A person said, "Speak to us about love.")
gibran: "Then said Almitra, Speak to us of _Love_."

phirae miona miso koma to ka rihe. shia lo miona to somela. lo miona maeli to kelu. shia roe whalo haoni to haolu.
different person REFL head PST CAUS rise. 3SG PL person PST observe. PL person quiet PST become. 3SG INS large voice PST speak.
(A different person raised his head and observed the people. The people became quiet. He spoke with a great voice.)
gibran: "And he raised his head and looked upon the people, and there fell a stillness upon them. And with a great voice he said:"

lu lothea thia whola. thia ha whola po kelomi. thia ha whola po naweri.
COND love 2SG invite. 2SG PROX invite POT accept. 2SG PROX invite POT refuse.
(When love invites you, you may accept this invitation. You may refuse it.)
gibran: "When love beckons to you, follow him,"

whekai lo lothea ruela kethua po nai. lo shia ruela reshi po rihe. thia po therilu.
CONTR PL love path hard POT be. PL 3SG path fast POT rise. 2SG POT rest.
(However, love's paths may be hard. Its paths may rise quickly. You may rest.)
gibran: "Though his ways are hard and steep."

lu lothea lo wapile thia lomare. thia ha lomare po lesawi. thia ha lomare po naweri.
COND love PL wing 2SG embrace. 2SG PROX embrace POT consent. 2SG PROX embrace POT refuse.
(When love's wings embrace you, you may consent to this embrace. You may refuse it.)
gibran: "And when his wings enfold you yield to him,"

lu tiso thena thoa lo wapile se kupe. no pukea moreko. no pholeni.
COND sharp thing AMONG PL wing PASS hide. IMP danger articulate. IMP depart.
(If a sharp thing is hidden among the wings, put the danger into words. Leave.)
gibran: "Though the sword hidden among his pinions may wound you."
```

**Notes:** Love still opens the scene, but an invitation does not carry its own answer. The listener may accept or refuse both the path and the embrace. Difficulty does not cancel rest. When the wing conceals something sharp, the transmutation stops treating the wound as love's private mystery. `moreko` gives the danger exact words, and `pholeni` tells the listener to leave. The source's sword sits beside the passage; Phi does not disguise it with a gentler name.

### Voice, garden, branch, and root

```
lu lothea wei thia haolu. no shia haolu theonu. thia lila ha haolu nohero ralu nai. thia lila ha haolu naweri ralu nai.
COND love DAT 2SG speak. IMP 3SG speak attend. 2SG PURP PROX speak believe free be. 2SG PURP PROX speak refuse free be.
(When love speaks to you, attend to its speech. You are free to believe it. You are free to refuse it.)
gibran: "And when he speaks to you believe in him,"

lothea haoni lo thia whemura po helui. thepalu lue nitho howeli po pukeri. pukeri lothea shewo ka ma kelu.
love voice PL 2SG dream POT change. garden ABL north wind POT damage. damage love true CAUS NEG become.
(Love's voice may change your dreams. A garden may be damaged by the north wind. Damage does not make love true.)
gibran: "Though his voice may shatter your dreams as the north wind lays waste the garden."

lothea thia po keluro. kaworu lothea siru ma nai. lothea thia thuroa po panoru. lu loremi mokela nai. thesa kati phireo po panoru.
love 2SG POT honor. injury love marker NEG be. love 2SG grow POT support. COND branch sick be. careful cut plant POT support.
(Love may honour you. Injury is not a marker of love. Love may support your growth. When a branch is sick, careful cutting may support the plant.)
gibran: "For even as love crowns you so shall he crucify you. Even as he is for your growth so is he for your pruning."

lothea kau thia raeli kalei. shia lo [whu mua sileta phelo tiripe] mo ko phaeli loremi moli palo.
love ALL 2SG height climb. 3SG PL [REL LOC sun light quiver] CMPR FOC tender branch gentle touch.
(Love climbs to your height. It gently touches the most tender branches that quiver in sunlight.)
gibran: "Even as he ascends to your height and caresses your tenderest branches that quiver in the sun,"

lo thia tumeki muila kema phelu. lothea lo tumeki theonu.
PL 2SG root earth strong hold. love PL root attend.
(Your roots hold the earth strongly. Love attends to the roots.)
gibran: "So shall he descend to your roots and shake them in their clinging to the earth."

lo miona mua lothea nuawe sholei.
PL person LOC love together gather.
(People gather together in love.)
gibran: "Like sheaves of corn he gathers you unto himself."
```

**Notes:** Instead of required belief, the listener attends. The words may be believed or refused, and that freedom is grammatical rather than tucked into a disclaimer. A voice can change a dream, and wind can damage a garden; the damage proves nothing about love. Crown and crucifixion separate into honour and injury, with `kaworu lothea siru ma nai` refusing their old equivalence. A sick branch may be cut carefully in support of the plant. Upper branches and lower roots belong to one living form, but love attends rather than shakes. People gather as people, not as sheaves.

### Grain and the shared table

```
lo miona napuro kema palo. lo shia lue napuro lo tawemi leiro.
PL person grain strong touch. PL 3SG ABL grain PL cover release.
(People make forceful contact with grain. They release its coverings.)
gibran: "He threshes you to make you naked."

lo shia thue mera napuro ka roke. lo napuro leko tawemi lue napuro pholeni.
PL 3SG THROUGH net grain CAUS move. PL grain surface cover ABL grain depart.
(They move grain through a net. The grain's outer coverings depart from it.)
gibran: "He sifts you to free you from your husks."

lo shia napuro muo whilo kuremi.
PL 3SG grain INTO white grind.
(They grind the grain into whiteness.)
gibran: "He grinds you to whiteness."

lo shia napuro nela phialu welotu. lo shia ha welotu thena lupaki. lo shia ha welotu thena pesa. ha welotu thena luwi kelu.
PL 3SG grain COORD water mix. PL 3SG PROX mix thing fold. PL 3SG PROX mix thing push. PROX mix thing flexible become.
(They mix grain and water. They fold the mixture and press it. The mixture becomes flexible.)
gibran: "He kneads you until you are pliant;"

lila sila mua holia napa nuawe nuola lo shia muo moeli thero ha welotu thena sepho. ha welotu thena napa kelu.
PURP community LOC festival bread together eat PL 3SG INTO hearth fire PROX mix thing send. PROX mix thing bread become.
(So that the community may eat bread together at a festival, they send the mixture into the hearth fire. The mixture becomes bread.)
gibran: "And then he assigns you to his sacred fire, that you may become sacred bread for God’s sacred feast."

lu miona lothea. shia miso korua lo kupela po sano. sheno shia mua lioru po pawela.
COND person love. 3SG REFL heart PL secret POT know. ADD 3SG LOC life POT participate.
(When a person loves, they may know the secrets of their own heart. They may also participate in life.)
gibran: "All these things shall love do unto you that you may know the secrets of your heart, and in that knowledge become a fragment of Life’s heart."
```

**Notes:** Gibran's grain-floor survives, though grain rather than a person receives the work. People work the grain; love does not work the people. Forceful contact loosens the covering, and the net separates it. Stones grind the grain white; hands mix, fold, and press. The purpose comes first: bread is for a community eating together at a festival. The fire belongs to a hearth, not a sacred trial, and the person participates in life instead of being made a fragment of another heart. Transformation needs no human raw material.

### Fear and freedom

```
lu thia sukima phaelo. no ha sukima moreko. thia shea nela nirelo po kanu.
COND 2SG fear feel. IMP PROX fear articulate. 2SG peace COORD pleasant POT choose.
(When you feel fear, put this fear into words. You may choose peace and pleasure.)
gibran: "But if in your fear you would seek only love’s peace and love’s pleasure,"

thia lila manolu ralu nai. thia lila pholeni ralu nai.
2SG PURP stay free be. 2SG PURP depart free be.
(You are free to stay. You are free to leave.)
gibran: "Then it is better for you that you cover your nakedness and pass out of love’s threshing-floor,"

lu thia pholeni. thia lo meluna po hekawi. thia hola whoa po nai. thia nuhewa whoa po nai.
COND 2SG depart. 2SG PL season POT find. 2SG laugh whole POT be. 2SG weep whole POT be.
(If you leave, you may find the seasons. Your laughter may be whole. Your weeping may be whole.)
gibran: "Into the seasonless world where you shall laugh, but not all of your laughter, and weep, but not all of your tears."

lothea li miso loa. lothea li lue miso pilu.
love RESTR REFL give. love RESTR ABL REFL take.
(Love gives only itself; love takes only from itself.)
gibran: "Love gives naught but itself and takes naught but from itself."

lothea ma phelu. lothea se ma phelu.
love NEG hold. love PASS NEG hold.
(Love holds nothing, and is not held.)
gibran: "Love possesses not nor would it be possessed;"

lothea lothea heno.
love love satisfy.
(Love satisfies love.)
gibran: "For love is sufficient unto love."
```

**Notes:** Fear is testimony here, not a failed entrance examination. The speaker puts it into words and may choose peace or pleasure without being sent into an empty season. Staying and leaving are both free acts. If the person leaves, the seasons are still available, and neither laughter nor tears are made partial as a penalty. Gibran's next three claims pass through without dispute: love gives only itself, refuses possession, and is enough for love.

### Guidance and desire

```
lu thia lothea. no sha liona mua mia korua nai sho ma haolu. no sha mia mua liona korua nai sho haolu.
COND 2SG love. IMP QUOT.COMP live LOC 1SG heart be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE NEG speak. IMP QUOT.COMP 1SG LOC live heart be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE speak.
(When you love, do not say, "Life is in my heart." Say, "I am in the heart of Life.")
gibran: "When you love you should not say, “God is in my heart,” but rather, “I am in the heart of God.”"

thia lothea po ma kulo. lu lothea tha thia henoi nai tho hekawi. lothea thia kulo.
2SG love POT NEG guide. COND love DECL.COMP 2SG ENOUGH be DECL.COMP.CLOSE find. love 2SG guide.
(You cannot guide love. If love finds that you are enough, love guides you.)
gibran: "And think not you can direct the course of love, for love, if it finds you worthy, directs your course."

lothea li tha miso pheno kelu tho pula.
love RESTR DECL.COMP REFL full become DECL.COMP.CLOSE wish.
(Love wishes only that it become full.)
gibran: "Love has no other desire but to fulfil itself."

lu thia lothea nela lo pula phelu. su ha lo pula thia lo pula nai.
COND 2SG love COORD PL wish hold. OPT PROX PL wish 2SG PL wish be.
(If you love and hold desires, may these desires be your desires.)
gibran: "But if you love and must needs have desires, let these be your desires:"

su thia phea thiku luphore kelu. su thia selu. su thia wei shero miso melira meliho.
OPT 2SG AS small river become. OPT 2SG flow. OPT 2SG DAT night REFL song sing.
(May you become like a small river. May you flow. May you sing your own song to the night.)
gibran: "To melt and be like a running brook that sings its melody to the night."
```

**Notes:** Life occupies the divine heart's place. The earlier fragment already points there. The person is enough, not graded for entry. Guidance accompanies a path without taking control of it. Love may seek its own fullness, but the desires belong to the speaker. The brook has its flow and night-song; it need not melt before it can move.

### Tenderness without tribute

```
su thia phaeli lothea sano. lu kipona shua. no ha kipona moreko.
OPT 2SG tender love know. COND pain come. IMP PROX pain articulate.
(May you know tender love. When pain comes, put this pain into words.)
gibran: "To know the pain of too much tenderness."

su thia lothea nulo shelomu. kaworu shelomu siru ma nai.
OPT 2SG love deep understand. injury understand marker NEG be.
(May you understand love deeply. Injury is not a marker of understanding.)
gibran: "To be wounded by your own understanding of love;"

lothea thia lorika ma phelona. su thia roe pula nela siora miso loa.
love 2SG blood NEG require. OPT 2SG INS wish COORD joy REFL give.
(Love does not require your blood. May you give yourself willingly and joyfully.)
gibran: "And to bleed willingly and joyfully."

su thia mua horathe roe wapile korua rihe. su thia lao we ta lothea philo liona whaline.
OPT 2SG LOC dawn INS wing heart rise. OPT 2SG BECAUSE ALSO one love day live thank.
(May you rise at dawn with a wing-heart, and thank Life for one more day of loving.)
gibran: "To wake at dawn with a winged heart and give thanks for another day of loving;"

su thia mua philo kesho therilu. su thia mua lothea whalo siora phirena.
OPT 2SG LOC day middle rest. OPT 2SG LOC love large joy meditate.
(May you rest at the day's middle, and meditate within love's great joy.)
gibran: "To rest at the noon hour and meditate love’s ecstacy;"

su thia mua howai nemo kau womu turema.
OPT 2SG LOC evening grateful ALL home return.
(May you return home at evening with gratitude.)
gibran: "To return home at eventide with gratitude;"

su thia nulae. su mua thia korua wei [whu thia meropi] miona thorai nai. su nia thia phulae ta woraka meliho nai.
OPT 2SG sleep. OPT LOC 2SG heart DAT [REL 2SG cherish] person bless be. OPT ON 2SG mouth one appreciate sing be.
(Then may you sleep, with a blessing in your heart for the person you cherish and a song of appreciation upon your lips.)
gibran: "And then to sleep with a prayer for the beloved in your heart and a song of praise upon your lips."
```

**Notes:** Tenderness has no need of pain as its measure. When pain arrives, `moreko` makes it speakable. Understanding can deepen without injury, and love asks for no blood. The gift freely offered is the self rather than a wound. Dawn, noon, evening, and sleep follow Gibran's daily arc. The transmutation thanks Life, rests with love's great joy, blesses the cherished person, and lets the final song praise what the singer actually appreciates.

## What changes between the two renderings

The close translation lets Gibran's love do everything the source requires of it, from commanding and wounding to crucifying and judging the fearful departure. It also puts the lover through threshing and grinding. The transmutation follows the hard path and transforming grain, but changes who chooses the path and what enters the mill. Read together, the two renderings separate an image of love that sanctifies suffering from one that can meet pain without claiming it as proof.

### Solarpunk values

Garden branches and roots place love in an ecology. Grain meets water, hearth, and bread to give it a material life. In the close rendering, those forms hold Gibran's account of surrender and transformation. The transmutation puts the grain into many hands and the bread on a shared table. Love is still intimate, but it is no longer private weather acting on an isolated body.

### Secular Buddhist philosophy

Attention comes before belief. Fear and pain are noticed and put into words without being made into failures of courage or stages of purification. The natural day offers four places to return: dawn, the day's middle, evening, and sleep. None requires the speaker to pretend that pain is wisdom.

### Art Nouveau aesthetics

Gibran's curves survive almost untouched. Wings fold around a body while branches quiver in sunlight and roots clasp earth. Water leaves the solid shape; bread changes under the hand. The close translation lets those lines tighten around sword and cross. The transmutation opens them again at the consenting body and the communal table.

### Peace linguistics

The close rendering describes the sword and crucifixion plainly because accuracy cannot hide them. It coins no weapon or execution root. The transmutation refuses the claim that injury marks love, makes hidden danger speakable, and leaves departure available. Its strongest sentence is ordinary: `lothea thia lorika ma phelona.` Love does not require your blood.

### Pre-industrial wisdom

The grain sequence belongs to work people knew through the hand. Hands loosen the covering, pass grain through a net, and grind it. Then they mix it with water, fold it, press it, and place it near fire. Gibran turns that labour upon the lover. Phi returns it to grain and shares the bread at a festival. The day passes by sunlight, not by an hour.

Gibran asks the lover to become sacred bread. Phi leaves the person at the table, whole enough to eat.
