# thena whori phelu: The thing holds its mending

A storm takes down a garden wall. A parent repairs it with stone, and today the cat is walking along the top as if the masonry had settled the question already. Anyone standing in the garden can ask whether this is the same wall. As it stands, the question has no answer, and the reason it has none turns out to be the interesting part.

This is an original Phi essay. Phi is the source text, and the English lines follow it as close readings. The argument draws on optional vocabulary from Philosophical Reasoning, Commons and Collective Governance, Systems and Shared Infrastructure, and Work, Craft, and Repair where those distinctions earn their place; all remaining content vocabulary is base.

## The essay

```
kurisha to shua. moru to lepa. phao roe kerou moru to shiroka.
storm PST come. wall PST fall. parent INS stone wall PST repair.
(A storm came. The wall fell. The parent repaired the wall with stones.)

misheko ha philo nia moru leko thalo.
cat PROX day ON wall surface walk.
(Today the cat walks on the wall's surface.)

wa ha moru senao nai.
Q PROX wall same be.
(Is this wall the same wall?)

ha thilou sholu ma nai. senao kirema phelona.
PROX inquire complete NEG be. same criterion require.
(This question is not complete. Sameness requires a criterion.)

lu miona kirema ma kanu. ha thilou remuma ma phelu.
COND person criterion NEG choose. PROX inquire conclusion NEG hold.
(If a person chooses no criterion, this question holds no conclusion.)

lu kirema kerou nai. moru phirae nai. lo newu kerou mua moru nai.
COND criterion stone be. wall different be. PL new stone LOC wall be.
(If the criterion is stone, the wall is different. New stones are in the wall.)

lu kirema moru kelitho nai. moru senao nai. moru mua senao lokue manolu. moru thepalu norui nai.
COND criterion wall function be. wall same be. wall LOC same place stay. wall garden boundary be.
(If the criterion is the wall's function, the wall is the same. The wall stays in the same place. The wall is the garden's boundary.)

ha wi sherewa telua nai. ha wi sherewa wiso ma phiketu. senao mua kirema nai.
PROX two claim accurate be. PROX two claim RECP NEG contradict. same LOC criterion be.
(These two claims are accurate. These two claims do not contradict each other. Sameness is in a criterion.)

lo miona nia moru leko ro thalo. kerou rohemi. ha rohemi pukeri ma nai.
PL person ON wall surface HAB walk. stone wear down. PROX wear down damage NEG be.
(People walk on the wall's surface habitually. The stone wears down. This wearing down is not damage.)

rohemi thena luera thesori nai.
wear down thing past evidence be.
(Wearing down is evidence of the thing's past.)

lu miona thena talome. thena wea luera kire roke. whekai ha luera kire noetha ma nai.
COND person thing restore. thing TOWARD past shape move. CONTR PROX past shape essential NEG be.
(If a person restores a thing, the thing moves toward a past shape. However, this past shape is not essential.)

miona ha kire kanu. thena ha kire ma kanu.
person PROX shape choose. thing PROX shape NEG choose.
(The person chooses this shape. The thing does not choose this shape.)

thena whori phelu.
thing mend hold.
(The thing holds its mending.)

whori mua thena luera nai. luera lue thena ma wepu.
mend LOC thing past be. past ABL thing NEG go.
(The mending is in the thing's past. The past does not leave the thing.)

moru roe whori ma pukeri.
wall INS mend NEG damage.
(The wall does not worsen through the mending.)

thena rolia phelu. miona ha rolia ma loa.
thing worth hold. person PROX worth NEG give.
(The thing holds worth. A person does not give this worth.)

soli miona whori ma sone. whekai ha sone thena rolia ma helui.
SOME person mend NEG value. CONTR PROX value thing worth NEG change.
(Some people do not value the mending. However, this valuing does not change the thing's worth.)

ha sherewa whakeru phelona.
PROX claim object require.
(This claim requires an objection.)

lu miona theula kerou helui. mawha thorui kerou mua moru manolu. wa moru senao manolu.
COND person UNIV stone change. NONE beginning stone LOC wall stay. Q wall same stay.
(If a person changes all the stones, no first stone stays in the wall. Does the wall stay the same?)

lu ha sherewa telua nai. mawha thena lumae phelu. ha remuma shewo ma nai.
COND PROX claim accurate be. NONE thing end hold. PROX conclusion true NEG be.
(If this claim is accurate, no thing holds an end. This conclusion is not true.)

mia ha whakeru kelomi. shorela mia sherewa hieru ka kelu.
1SG PROX object accept. REPH 1SG claim narrow CAUS become.
(I accept this objection. In other words, I make the claim narrow.)

lu thena pukate. thena whoa ma manolu. whekai ha pukate whori nela pilewa ma kanu.
COND thing break. thing whole NEG stay. CONTR PROX break mend COORD make NEG choose.
(If a thing breaks, it does not stay whole. However, this break does not choose between mending and making.)

whori nela pilewa senao ma nai.
mend COORD make same NEG be.
(Mending and making are not the same.)

lu thena kelitho manolu. whori mua thena luera nai.
COND thing function stay. mend LOC thing past be.
(If the thing's function remains, the mending is in the thing's past.)

whekai ta thilou manolu. sua kirema kanu.
CONTR one inquire stay. who criterion choose.
(However, one question stays. Who chooses the criterion?)

phao kerou kirema po kanu. sila moru kelitho kirema po kanu. lopia misheko lokue kirema po kanu.
parent stone criterion POT choose. community wall function criterion POT choose. child cat place criterion POT choose.
(The parent may choose the criterion of stone. The community may choose the criterion of the wall's function. The child may choose the criterion of the cat's place.)

lo shia sherewa telua nai. moru mawha kirema loa.
PL 3SG claim accurate be. wall NONE criterion give.
(Their claims are accurate. The wall gives no criterion.)

senao mua kirema nai. whekai rolia mua thena nai.
same LOC criterion be. CONTR worth LOC thing be.
(Sameness is in a criterion. However, worth is in the thing.)

lao ha lo mia wei moru rolia kirema ma phelona.
BECAUSE PROX PL 1SG DAT wall worth criterion NEG require.
(Because of this, we require no criterion for the wall's worth.)

ta thilou manolu. lu lo miona wiso ma nawo. sua kanu.
one inquire stay. COND PL person RECP NEG agree. who choose.
(One question stays. If people do not agree with each other, who chooses?)

misheko ha thilou ma phelu. misheko nia moru leko thalo. moru misheko panoru.
cat PROX inquire NEG hold. cat ON wall surface walk. wall cat support.
(The cat holds no such question. The cat walks on the wall's surface. The wall supports the cat.)
```

## Proposition record

| Move | Proposition or distinction | State at the close |
|---|---|---|
| Opening question | Is the rebuilt wall the same wall? | Held open, then shown to be incomplete as asked. |
| Incompleteness | Sameness requires a criterion, so a question without one reaches no conclusion. | Accepted, and it governs everything after it. |
| Two answers | By the criterion of stone the wall differs; by the criterion of its function it is the same. Both statements are accurate and neither contradicts the other. | Held together, which is the point: the pair is consistent only because each answer names its criterion. |
| Wear | Wearing down is not damage. It is evidence of the thing's past. | The lexicon separates the two before the essay argues for it. |
| Restoration | Returning a thing toward an earlier shape does not make that shape essential. The person chooses the reference; the thing does not. | Stands, and it removes the assumption that the unbroken wall was the true one. |
| Thesis | The thing holds its mending: the mend enters the thing's past, and the past does not leave the thing. | Asserted, then narrowed. |
| Worth | Worth belongs to the thing rather than to an observer's approval, so declining to value a mend does not lower what the mended thing is worth. | Accepted and left standing. |
| Objection | If every mend stays with the thing, a wall rebuilt stone by stone stays the same wall, and nothing ever ends. | Granted as a real consequence of the unnarrowed claim. |
| Revision | A complete break settles wholeness, but it does not choose between mending and making. The claim narrows to a thing whose function remains: its mending belongs to its past. | The physical break no longer supplies the criterion that the essay says someone must choose. |
| Chooser | The parent, the community, and the child each may choose a criterion, and each resulting claim may be accurate. The wall supplies none. | Left unresolved. |
| Asymmetry | Sameness sits in the criterion; worth sits in the thing. Only one of the two waits on someone's choice. | The essay's reasoned position. |
| Close | The unresolved case is disagreement about which criterion governs. | Named and left open. |

## Vocabulary under pressure

| Question | Phi treatment | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Can Phi ask the identity question without a noun for identity? | `senao` (same) already carries the respect under discussion inside its own definition, so the essay names that respect with `kirema` (criterion) and asks which one governs. `noetha` (essential) then says what would be indispensable to the thing under a criterion. | The question becomes sharper in Phi than in English, where a bare "the same" hides the criterion it depends on. |
| Do the repair verbs stay distinct in sustained use? | The wall takes `shiroka` (repair) as the primer's household has always used it, `whori` (mend) names the mending itself through the event-noun rule, `talome` (restore) supplies return toward a stated shape, and `pilewa` (make) remains distinct from both. | The four carry the argument without letting a physical break decide which act occurred. |
| Does the visible trace of a mend need a root? | The event-noun rule supplies the result reading, so `moru whori` is both the wall's mending and the mend it left. `thesori` (evidence) covers the reading of wear as a record of use. | No root is coined here. Repeated use across further work can decide whether a dedicated noun for the seam earns its place; the question is registered rather than left in conversation. |
| Can wear be described without implying decline? | `rohemi` (wear down) reports gradual change under use and says nothing about worsening, while `pukeri` (damage) reports the adverse change and `pukate` (break) the complete loss of wholeness. | Three words hold a distinction English usually leaves to tone. |
| Which optional distinctions earn their place? | Philosophical Reasoning supplies `phiketu` (contradict), `remuma` (conclusion), and shared Commons term `whakeru` (object). Systems and Work supply `kelitho` (function), which keeps a wall's operation apart from deliberate `phoa` (do). | Four optional roots carry six module memberships across four learning paths. The essay needs no new root. |

## Five-pillar reading

| Pillar | Work done in the essay |
|---|---|
| Solarpunk values | Repair is the ordinary case rather than the sad one. A rebuilt wall still performs its function, and the mend does not lessen its worth. |
| Secular Buddhist philosophy | Nothing in the wall holds still. The essay accepts that and locates sameness in a chosen criterion rather than in an essence the stones were supposed to be guarding. |
| Art Nouveau aesthetics | The argument stays in stone, surface, and the weight of feet. Its most abstract claim is settled by a cat who walks the top without waiting for the question to be answered. |
| Peace linguistics | The objection is stated at full strength and granted before it is answered. The revision names what the break settles and what it leaves for people to decide. |
| Pre-industrial wisdom | A parent repairs the wall with stone. Wear reads as a record of use rather than as a defect to be tidied away. |

The essay ends one step short of the question it raised. Sameness answers to whichever criterion a person names, and the wall itself supplies none, so two people may speak accurately and still disagree about which answer governs. Worth was never waiting on that argument. The cat, who has views on surfaces but none on criteria, was on the wall before the question began.
