# nophi lue mawha lokue — News from Nowhere, ch. 5: Children on the Road

*Beyond the market, Guest and Guide enter Kensington wood. Children have made a summer life among its tents and fires, and Guest soon asks the question he brought from his own century: when do they go back to school? The word opens beneath him. Guide knows children who swim and cook, who thatch roofs and work wood. They read, write, learn languages, and choose their own studies. What he does not know is why those acts should need an institution to make them count.*

*Morris's comedy survives, especially Guest's gift for finding a trap in an innocent noun. So does the harder argument beneath it. Practical skill uses thought. Reading need not arrive by compulsion, and a child's appetite for books need not become the measure of every child. The final turn enters Westminster's old buildings, where preservation and public use meet Dick's enthusiasm for beauty and architecture. Each unit gives the Phi passage, exact gloss, back-translation, and Morris's wording.*

**Notes:** Morris's place names stay in his lines. Phi begins with the wood's short southward reach, then follows it north and west. The north-east turn, narrow strip, and high ground above the wet plains come next. The neighbouring forest still holds out its hand. Morris names oak, sweet chestnut, plane, and sycamore; Phi uses the broader tree-kind where no direct species word exists and names the sycamore with `pilomu`. The arcade becomes a beautiful roof above the footpath with many stone arches. Morris supplies the plaster, with no invented colour or texture from Phi. The forest's "romance" is its wonder, and the naturalists are nature-scholars. At the end of the road, Guide smells green forest scents, chiefly `phero`, fern, rubbed underfoot.

**Notes:** The children range from six or eight to sixteen or seventeen, and Phi keeps every age. Guest sees especially fine bodily form and great enjoyment; the translation does not replace his judgement with an unsupported diagnosis of health. The tents, grass, fires, and hanging pots retain the camp's working shape. Morris's historical wording "gipsy fashion" stays visible without becoming a Phi category for people. Cottages are small homes that once held another name, in a society where some people were denied freedom. Summer parties stay for weeks and learn to act on their own. They go to the two named forests or the northern wild lands. Dick's regret is exact too: rough work has grown scarcer over the last fifty years.
