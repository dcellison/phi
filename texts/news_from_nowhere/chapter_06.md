# nophi lue mawha lokue — News from Nowhere, ch. 6: A Little Shopping

*Piccadilly first resembles an old shopping street closely enough to fool Guest's eyes. Beautiful things fill its windows, people enter and leave with parcels, and children mind one of the booths. Yet the resemblance fails wherever exchange should appear. Nobody weighs Guest's tobacco, names an amount, or understands why the carved pipe ought to require a payment. The children give what pleases them and expect the object to keep moving if its first holder loses it.*

*A darker conversation runs beneath the gifts. Guide remembers a recent loss caused by possessive love, then an old passenger joins the carriage and turns idleness into a mock disease inherited from people who once compelled others to work. Morris makes the satire cruel on purpose: class contempt and bodily judgement mingle with a joke about medicine. Phi puts each claim in its speaker's mouth. Coercion and death stay plain, while ridicule never gets to masquerade as diagnosis.*

**Notes:** Piccadilly is Morris's name. Phi follows what Guest can see: lower rooms arranged for exchange, things set before windows, parcels carried under people's arms, and a roof of arches above the footpath. The resemblance to commerce is his inference, and it lasts only while nobody asks what must be given in return. Buying, selling, and shopping therefore receive no borrowed verbs. Dick offers instead to let Guest choose and take a few things. Morris leaves Italy and the Houses of Parliament in his own words. The neighbouring market contains cabbages and turnips, along with beer and wine. Phi describes a distant old place, old buildings of authority, field foods, grain drink, and fruit drink. The guest-houses use registered `phemi womu`.
