# 30 · ko li

*Two last small words, and both of them work by pointing at whichever part of the sentence carries the weight.* **ko** *singles one out.* **li** *fences the claim to it and shuts the others out. The difference between those two jobs is the whole chapter:*

## I

> **lopia to shua.**
> **ko lopia to shua.**
> **li lopia to shua.**

| new word | say it | it means |
|---|---|---|
| ko | ko | (before a word: this one, as against the others) |
| li | li | (before a word: only) |

*The child came. It was the child who came. Only the child came. The first reports an arrival. The second answers a question about which of them it was. The third says something about everyone else.*

## II

> **ko mia thia nila.**
> **mia ko thia nila.**
> **mia thia ko nila.**

*I am the one who sees you. You are the one I see. Seeing is what I do to you. The words never move; only the little word does, and it lands wherever the weight belongs.*

## III

> **li wheo ha sano.**
> **li ta powea mua tomi nai.**
> **wheo li thia wei nophi loa.**

*Only the elder knows this. There is only one egg in the pot. The elder tells the story to you alone.* **li** *fences who and which and where; it never counts. One egg is one egg because the numeral says so.*

## IV

> **misheko li nuora rinu.**
> **lohau theula thena rinu.**

| new word | say it | it means |
|---|---|---|
| rinu | ri · nu | want |

*The cat wants only food. The dog wants everything. Two animals, two philosophies, and the difference between them is one particle.*

---

*That is the last of the particles. Thirty-eight small words, and not one of them ever changes shape. Each stands in front of whatever it affects. Part I promised you that on its first page; you have now met every word that keeps the promise.*

*The machinery, when you want it: focus* ko *and restriction* li *are the manual's Part IV, chapter 9, at the end of the Slot 2 inventory.*
