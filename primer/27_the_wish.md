# 27 · su

*Every sentence so far has reported something: what is, what was, what someone made happen.* **su** *does none of that. It stands at the very front, where* wa *and* no *and* lu *stand, and turns the whole sentence into a wish. Nothing is claimed. Something is hoped:*

## I

> **su thia welao nai.**
> **su thia wolu nai.**
> **su thia siora phelu.**
> **su lo thia shea phelu.**

| new word | say it | it means |
|---|---|---|
| su | su | (at the front: may it be, let it be) |
| wolu | wo · lu | healthy |
| shea | she · a | peace |

*May you be well. May you be healthy. May you have joy. May you all have peace. The sentences underneath are ordinary; the little word at the front changes what they are for.*

## II

> **su pheralu lepa.**
> **su thepalu thuroa.**
> **su lo shiro laeno thimu whano.**

*May the rain fall. May the garden grow. May the trees stand a long time. A wish can be spoken over a field as easily as over a person, which is most of what people have ever done with wishes.*

## III

> **wheo sha su thia hiso ruela phelu sho haolu.**
> **phao sha su thia womu wireo turema sho haolu.**

| new word | say it | it means |
|---|---|---|
| turema | tu · re · ma | return |

*The elder says: may you have a clear path. The parent says: may you come home later. This is what people say at a door, and Phi says it with the same word every time.*

## IV

> **su thia mia halemu.**
> **su mia thia halemu.**

| new word | say it | it means |
|---|---|---|
| halemu | ha · le · mu | remember |

*May you remember me. May I remember you. The two sentences are one word apart and the difference is the whole of what a parting is.*

---

*You now hold the fourth of the front particles.* wa *asks,* no *directs,* lu *supposes, and* su *wishes. None of them changes a single word behind it; each changes what the sentence is doing in the world.*

*The machinery, when you want it: the optative* su *is the manual's Part IV, chapter 9, with the other framing particles. It is also the mood the Metta Sutta runs on, which is waiting at the end of this book.*
