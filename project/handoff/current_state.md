The shelf's method distinction remains settled:

- A translation is answerable to every source proposition, relation, and image as closely as Phi permits.
- Translation moves in two isolated directions. Finish source to Phi first; then hide the source and derive English from frozen Phi alone. A Phi revision discards the affected English and restarts that direction.
- A source refusal may answer rather than reproduce a source, but it must say plainly that it is not a translation and must keep every departure visible.
- In an original, Phi is the source. English follows as a close reading, and the proposition record states what the work argues, revises, and leaves open.
- A source-led work declares its relationship to the source. Translation carries the source's claims; refusal answers without claiming equivalence. Original work is where Phi's own philosophy can begin without a prior author.
