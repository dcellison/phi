# Headless relative clauses

Sometimes context supplies the thing being described. In that case the **whu** clause occupies a noun-phrase position without an explicit head: "the one who" or "what."

## A referent supplied by context

```
whu mia to nila ha nai.
REL 1SG PST see PROX be.
(The one whom I saw is here.)
```

No noun follows the relative predicate **nila**. The complete headless phrase **whu mia to nila** is the subject of **ha nai**. Context must tell the listener whether the referent is a person or anything else that can be seen.

```
mia whu thia to kealo theo.
1SG REL 2SG PST create read.
(I read what you created.)
```

Here **whu thia to kealo** is the object of **theo**. English supplies "what"; Phi leaves the category open.

## Specific and general reference

A headless relative can point to one established referent or range over anyone or anything that fits the description. The surrounding sentence makes the difference:

```
whu thia to kealo mioru nai.
REL 2SG PST create beautiful be.
(What you created is beautiful.)
```

```
whu lo lopia thumela ha nai.
REL PL child teach PROX be.
(The one who teaches the children is here.)
```

```
whu thalo roke.
REL walk move.
(Whoever walks moves.)
```

The first example recovers a particular creation from the conversation. The second identifies a person by their present role without naming a permanent class. The third ranges over anyone who fits its description.

## `whu` remains

Dropping an established subject is ordinary topic-drop. Dropping **whu** is not the same operation. Without the relativizer, a listener receives content words but no grammatical signal that the whole clause occupies a noun-phrase position.

Every canonical headless relative therefore begins with **whu**. The word remains useful even in a short phrase because it distinguishes "what you created" from the ordinary statement "you created."

## Asking about identity

```
wa whu ha kealo thia nai.
Q REL PROX create 2SG be.
(Are you the one who created this?)
```

The headless phrase **whu ha kealo** is the subject, and **thia** is the identity being tested. The question names the relation to the action without assigning the referent a role noun.

A headed relative ends by naming its category. A headless relative declines to do so. Both keep **whu**, and both leave one role inside the description for the referent to fill.
