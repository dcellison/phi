# Complement clauses

A complement clause is a clause used as the object of another predicate. In "I know that they left," the departure is the thing known. Phi encloses that content with one of two matched pairs.

## Embedded statements: `tha ... tho`

**tha** opens an embedded statement and **tho** closes it before the main predicate. The pair reports propositional content rather than exact wording.

```
mia tha shia to wepu tho sano.
1SG DECL.COMP 3SG PST go DECL.COMP.CLOSE know.
(I know that they left.)
```

```
mia tha sileta sulae nai tho sano.
1SG DECL.COMP sun warm be DECL.COMP.CLOSE know.
(I know that the sun is warm.)
```

```
shia tha thia so kamo tho haolu.
3SG DECL.COMP 2SG FUT arrive DECL.COMP.CLOSE speak.
(They say that you will arrive.)
```

The embedded clause keeps ordinary word order and its own tense. After **tho**, the surrounding clause resumes with **sano** or **haolu**. Without the closer, the listener could not tell which predicate governs the framed content merely by waiting for a pause.

### Nesting

A declarative frame may contain another declarative frame. Each **tha** requires one **tho**, and the innermost pair closes first:

```
mia tha thia tha shia to wepu tho to haolu tho sano.
1SG DECL.COMP 2SG DECL.COMP 3SG PST go DECL.COMP.CLOSE PST speak DECL.COMP.CLOSE know.
(I know that you said that they left.)
```

The first **tho** completes "that they left." The past **haolu** then completes "you said that they left," and the second **tho** returns the sentence to **mia ... sano**. Nesting is regular, though a second sentence may be kinder to working memory when the frames begin to breed.

## Embedded yes-or-no questions: `pha ... pho`

**pha** turns a question with yes and no as possible answers into the object of a larger clause. **pho** closes that question before the main predicate.

```
mia pha shia to wepu pho phaelo.
1SG INT.COMP 3SG PST go INT.COMP.CLOSE feel.
(I wonder whether they left.)
```

```
shia pha mia so kamo pho ma sano.
3SG INT.COMP 1SG FUT arrive INT.COMP.CLOSE NEG know.
(They do not know whether I will arrive.)
```

```
shia pha mia to nila pho to thilou.
3SG INT.COMP 1SG PST see INT.COMP.CLOSE PST inquire.
(They asked whether I had seen.)
```

The embedded question never takes **wa**. **wa** frames a direct question addressed to someone; **pha ... pho** makes the unresolved yes-or-no proposition part of a statement.

## Embedded content questions

A content question already contains the gap-word that identifies what is unknown. The interrogative stands where its answer would stand, and no **pha ... pho** frame is used.

```
mia sua to wepu sano.
1SG who PST go know.
(I know who left.)
```

```
mia ne sulae kua nai ma sano.
1SG NAME sulae where be NEG know.
(I do not know where sulae is.)
```

```
shia thela peloru thuroa shelomu.
3SG how flower grow understand.
(They understand how the flowers grow.)
```

The surrounding clauses are statements. **sua**, **kua**, and **thela** occupy the unknown roles inside their embedded content, while the final predicates say what the matrix subjects know or understand.
