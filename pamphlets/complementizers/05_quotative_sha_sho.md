# Quotative embedding: `sha`/`sho`

Some words should arrive exactly as they were spoken. `sha` and `sho` are Phi's spoken quotation marks: everything between them is someone else's wording, preserved sound for sound, and carrying it is a small act of trust. Where `tha`/`tho` reports what someone meant, `sha`/`sho` carries what someone said.

## The basic structure

The structure for quotative embedding is:

```
[Subject] sha [Quoted material] sho [Verb of speaking or receiving speech]
```

Everything between `sha` and `sho` is preserved exactly as spoken. The main verb follows `sho`.

**Basic example:**

```
shia sha mia wepu sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I am leaving.")
```

Breaking this down:
- `shia`: main clause subject (the one who spoke)
- `sha`: opens quotation
- `mia wepu`, quoted material: "I am leaving"
- `sho`: closes quotation
- `haolu`: main verb (speak)

Note: the `mia` inside the quote refers to the original speaker, not to whoever is reporting the quote now.

## Sound symbolism

**`sha`** begins with `sh` /ʃ/, air hissing along a groove in the tongue: speech overheard through a wall, or a person drawing breath to say something. It carries further than any other fricative Phi has, which is what a quotation wants at its edges. The `a` opens, and someone else's words come through.

**`sho`** begins on the same hiss, but the lips are already rounded for it and stay rounded straight through `o`, so the closer is spoken with one shape of the mouth from end to end. The ear stays inside the quotation until that vowel turns.

`sha` lends your mouth to someone else. For as long as the frame stays open, the words you make are not yours; `sho` ends the loan, and your own voice comes back.

## The sacred trust of quotation

Phi treats exact quotation as a responsibility. When you use `sha`/`sho`, you commit to preserving the original speaker's words without alteration. Misquotation causes real harm: disagreements turn on what was actually said, and testimony is only as good as its carrying. By building the line between quotation and paraphrase into the grammar, Phi asks a speaker to notice, every time, which of the two they are doing. Whether they notice is theirs to bring; the question is asked in every frame.

The quoted words remain their speaker's own, and the frame says so out loud. There is a quiet humility in choosing it: paraphrase presumes understanding, while quotation admits that the original words may matter more than our reading of them. A community that carries its stories and its promises by voice needs exactly this, quotation marks that weigh nothing and cannot be lost between teller and listener.

## Quotation vs. content reporting

This distinction is fundamental:

**Quotation (`sha`/`sho`):** Preserves exact words.
```
shia sha mia wepu sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I am leaving.")
```

The `mia` is the original speaker's word. They called themselves `mia`.

**Content reporting (`tha`/`tho`):** Conveys meaning with shifted reference.
```
shia tha shia wepu tho to haolu
3SG DECL.COMP 3SG go DECL.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said that they were leaving.)
```

The `shia` refers to the original speaker, named from the reporter's perspective. The pronouns shift to keep reference straight.

**Practical difference:**

Imagine they said: "I love you" to someone.

Quotation:
```
shia sha mia thia lothea sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG 2SG love QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I love you.")
```

Content reporting:
```
shia tha shia thia lothea tho to haolu
3SG DECL.COMP 3SG 2SG love DECL.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said that they love you.)
```

In the quotation, `mia` and `thia` are exactly what they said. In the content report, we translate the speaker's self-reference to `shia` and keep `thia` as the addressee from our perspective.

## Verbs that take `sha`/`sho` clauses

The frame closes into one of Phi's speech verbs: `haolu` (speak), `shemui` (utter), `thilou` (inquire), or, for words received rather than produced, `hea` (hear). Hearing exact words is as sayable as speaking them; the closer bounds the quote either way.

### Saying

```
shia sha sileta mioru nai sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP sun beautiful be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "The sun is beautiful.")
```

```
melu sha lo mia so turema sho to haolu
friend QUOT.COMP PL 1SG FUT return QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(The friend said: "We will return.")
```

### Asking (direct question quoted)

```
shia sha wa thia towe nai sho to thilou
3SG QUOT.COMP Q 2SG well be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST inquire
(They asked: "Are you well?")
```

Note: The direct question particle `wa` appears inside the quotation because it was part of what they actually said.

### Announcing, declaring, proclaiming

```
miona sha mia ha nai sho to shemui
person QUOT.COMP 1SG PROX be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST utter
(The person declared: "I am here.")
```

### Manner of speaking

A descriptor before the verb says how the words were delivered:

```
shia sha mia wepu sho to shena haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST calm speak
(They said calmly: "I am leaving.")
```

## Quoting phrases and fragments

Not all quotations are complete sentences. `sha`/`sho` can frame any linguistic material:

### Single words

```
shia sha shewo sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP true QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "True.")
```

### Phrases

```
melu sha mioru sileta sho to haolu
friend QUOT.COMP beautiful sun QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(The friend said: "Beautiful sun.")
```

### Commands and exclamations

```
shia sha no shua sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP IMP come QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "Come!")
```

```
melu sha wao sho to haolu
friend QUOT.COMP wow QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(The friend exclaimed: "Wow!")
```

## Quoting questions

When the quoted material is a question, the question particle `wa` appears inside the quotation:

### Yes/no questions

```
shia sha wa thia wepu sho to thilou
3SG QUOT.COMP Q 2SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST inquire
(They asked: "Are you leaving?")
```

### Content questions

```
melu sha sua ha nai sho to thilou
friend QUOT.COMP who PROX be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST inquire
(The friend asked: "Who is here?")
```

```
shia sha thia hina nila sho to thilou
3SG QUOT.COMP 2SG what see QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST inquire
(They asked: "What do you see?")
```

Compare with embedded questions using `pha`/`pho`:

```
shia pha thia wepu pho to thilou
3SG INT.COMP 2SG go INT.COMP.CLOSE PST inquire
(They asked whether you were leaving.)
```

The `pha`/`pho` version reports the question's content. The `sha`/`sho` version preserves the exact words.

## Extended quotations

Quotations can be as long as needed. Everything between `sha` and `sho` is quoted material:

```
shia sha mia so wepu. mia kau womu na turema. no thia towe nai sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG FUT go. 1SG ALL home NEC return. IMP 2SG well be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I will leave. I must return home. Be well.")
```

The `sho` closes all the quoted material, however long.

## Quotation within quotation

What if someone quotes someone else quoting someone? Each level needs its own `sha`/`sho` pair:

```
mia sha thia sha mia so turema sho to haolu sho to hea
1SG QUOT.COMP 2SG QUOT.COMP 1SG FUT return QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST hear
(I heard: "You said: 'I will return.'")
```

This is complex but parseable. Each opener matches its closer:

```
mia sha [...sha...sho...] sho to hea
    └────────────────────────┘
              └─────────┘
```

In practice, such nesting is rare, but the structure supports it.

## Quotation with different tenses in the frame

The quoted material preserves its original form. The frame (main clause) can have any tense:

### Past frame

```
shia sha mia wepu sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I am leaving.")
```

The quoted material remains as they originally spoke it, while `to` on the frame verb, after the closer, marks that the saying happened in the past.

### Future frame

```
shia sha mia so turema sho so haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG FUT return QUOT.COMP.CLOSE FUT speak
(They will say: "I will return.")
```

## Topic-drop with `sha`/`sho`

When the speaker is clear from context:

**Full form:**
```
shia sha mia wepu sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I am leaving.")
```

**With topic-drop:**
```
sha mia wepu sho to haolu
QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
((They) said: "I am leaving.")
```

The quoted material remains intact.

## Quotation in questions

The main clause can question the quotation:

```
wa shia sha mia wepu sho to haolu
Q 3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(Did they say: "I am leaving"?)
```

```
wa thia sha shewo sho to hea
Q 2SG QUOT.COMP true QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST hear
(Did you hear (someone say): "True"?)
```

## Quotation with negation

Negating that someone said something:

```
shia sha mia wepu sho to ma haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST NEG speak
(They did not say: "I am leaving.")
```

Negation inside the quote (if that's what was said):

```
shia sha mia ma wepu sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG NEG go QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I am not leaving.")
```

## When to use quotation vs. content reporting

Choose based on what matters:

**Use `sha`/`sho` when:**
- Exact wording matters legally, spiritually, or personally
- You want to preserve the original speaker's voice
- Pronouns and deixis should remain as originally spoken
- You are quoting a question, command, or exclamation
- You are teaching someone what to say

**Use `tha`/`tho` when:**
- You are conveying meaning rather than form
- Reference needs to shift for clarity
- You are summarizing rather than transcribing
- The exact words are unknown or unimportant

Neither is "better." They serve different communicative purposes.

## Extended examples in context

### Teaching speech

```
no sha mia thia melu nai sho haolu. mia thia melu nai
IMP QUOT.COMP 1SG 2SG friend be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE speak. 1SG 2SG friend be
(Say: "I am your friend." I am your friend.)
```

The teacher quotes the words to be learned, then uses regular speech.

### Legal or sacred contexts

```
shewo nophi sha no wei theula miona lothea loa. no theula melu naphe sho haolu
true story QUOT.COMP IMP DAT UNIV person love give. IMP UNIV friend help QUOT.COMP.CLOSE speak
(The true story says: "Give love to all people. Help all friends.")
```

Preserving sacred text exactly as transmitted.

### Reporting a conversation

```
shia sha wa thia towe nai sho to thilou. mia sha mia towe nai sho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP Q 2SG well be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST inquire. 1SG QUOT.COMP 1SG well be QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They asked: "Are you well?" I said: "I am well.")
```

Recounting dialogue with exact words preserved.

### Contrasting quotation and content

```
shia sha mia thia lothea sho to haolu. shia tha shia mia lothea tho to haolu
3SG QUOT.COMP 1SG 2SG love QUOT.COMP.CLOSE PST speak. 3SG DECL.COMP 3SG 1SG love DECL.COMP.CLOSE PST speak
(They said: "I love you." They said that they love me.)
```

Both sentences report the same event, but:
- The first preserves the speaker's words exactly
- The second translates the meaning with shifted pronouns

## Summary

`sha`/`sho` is Phi's mechanism for exact quotation: another person's words preserved whole, in their own voice. Key points:

- **`sha` opens:** "here begins exact quotation"
- **`sho` closes:** "here ends that quotation"
- **Main verb follows:** the verb of speaking, or of receiving speech (`hea`)

Distinguished from `tha`/`tho`:
- Quotation preserves exact words and original pronouns
- Content reporting conveys meaning with shifted reference

The system handles any length of quoted material. Nesting is possible. Questions, commands, and fragments can all be quoted.

`sha` marks where your voice ends and another's begins; `sho` gives the voice back.
